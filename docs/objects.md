# Shared enemies and objects: research and protocol proposal

Plan stage 5 ([plan.md](plan.md)): bombs, arrows and other projectiles seen on every screen, one owner per enemy with a real hand-over, and hits on a copy sent to the owner as damage events. This page says which actors the game uses, where their state is, how an actor is told apart on two games, and proposes the messages and the ownership rules. No product code yet.

Addresses are offsets into `main` of build `e0f3ccfaf50065ae` (1.1.1). **Checked** means read from the instructions or the data files. **Inferred** means taken from a name, a shape or the engine family. **Live** means seen in the running game; see "Live check", the only part that was run.

## Results

- Every player projectile is its own actor, created through the same function as everything else (0x372a70, [puppet.md](puppet.md)). Shot ones go through the shootable create at **0xc16cf0**, which passes the blackboard values `Shootable_KeyHash` and `Shootable_OverrideParamNum` with the request.
- The actors: `NormalArrow` (arrows, also the actor fire, ice and light arrows are shot as: inferred), `Shootable_DekuSeed` (slingshot), `Shootable_Hook` and `Shootable_HookLong` (hookshot and longshot tip), `Item_Bomb`, `Item_Bombchu`, `Item_DekuNut`, `Weapon_Boomerang`. The `Obj_*` and `Item_Arrow_*` packs of the same names are pick-ups.
- An actor's name is a `char*` at actor **+0x260**. Its life is an s32 at **`*(Life + 0x1c10) + 8`**, its maximum at `*(Life + 0x1c18) + 8`, where Life is component-table entry **+0x2b8** (table at actor +0x270).
- A placed actor's 64-bit placement hash, the key of its flag in [flags.md](flags.md), is a u64 at **`*(actor + 0x3d8) + 0x88`**. A game-made actor has `0xffffffffffffffff` there (live).
- Live (2026-11-08): the actor list is found (`[[main + 0x4e91888] + 0x118] + 0x670`, node at actor + 0x538); `Shootable_DekuSeed` is confirmed; the life chain reads right on every enemy; damage is applied by the Life component's step 0x49d8c0, and writing life directly holds and runs the game's own death.
- Proposal: projectiles stay with the game that made them for their whole life and are drawn elsewhere as harmless copies; enemies have one owner with a fight lock and a hand-over that carries the state; any life lost on a copy goes to the owner as `DAMAGE`. Eight new messages (below).

### Damage (how life changes)

- **The function.** The Life component's step **0x49d8c0** applies damage. Each frame it sums what was dealt to the actor into **Life + 0x1cb0** (from **Life + 0x228**, **Life + 0x1350** and one more term), then sets life with a compare-and-swap at **0x49dcf8**: life minus that sum, at most max, never below 0. A GDB write watchpoint on an enemy's life stops there every frame, with sum 0 when nothing hit.
- **Life + 0x228** is a per-frame damage accumulator: 0xaa920 (called from 0x2c23e28) swaps it to 0 each frame. What adds a hit into it was not found: there is no atomic add to that offset in the code, so it is written through a base other than the Life component. A watchpoint cannot follow it live: the field is written several hundred times a second and each stop starves the frame loop (52,000 stops, no frame shown, in 90 s).
- **Life + 0x1ca8 and + 0x1cac** are a mirror of life and max, rewritten 1 to 2.5 s after life changes (0x8d96a0). They read -1 for an instant now and then. They do not guard life.
- **A real hit.** A player killed a Poe with the Kokiri Sword while a logger read it: life 3 to 2 to 1 to 0, one per hit, and the sum at Life + 0x1cb0 read 1 after each. 2 s after life reached 0 the Poe left the list and `Obj_Spirit_Poe` (its soul) appeared; its lantern `Weapon_PoeCantera` went with it. That Poe was made by the game near a dug-up grave (hash `0xffffffffffffffff`), so it is a game-made enemy: the "Made by the scene with no hash" row of the id table applies to it.
- **A direct write holds and kills.** Through the GDB stub: a ReDead's life 12 to 5 stayed 5, and its mirror followed. 5 to 0: 2.4 s later an `Obj_Rupee_Green` appeared at the ReDead's place, the game's own death drop. The ReDead stayed in the list in state 4, life 0, for the 20 s it was watched. So the owner can apply `DAMAGE` by writing life (the proposal's plan), and the game's own death follows; a copy's life must not be written to 0 by anyone but its owner.

### Placement hash (confirmed)

`*(actor + 0x3d8) + 0x88` is a u64 on every placed enemy, and the same after a reload: ReDead `0xf9b880ab096a9cd6`, Gold Skulltula `0x32f42f523be6425`, Keese `0x62a0218c021047f1`, `0x9b68efc6a861c030`, `0x30843dad7976f0fa`, `0xb8a209d8a2f24c4d`. Actors the game made (Link, the camera, the Poe) have **`0xffffffffffffffff`** there, not 0.

### States seen

4 for every awake actor. The Gold Skulltula went to **6** while Link was about 25 m away and left the list later, with life still 1 before that; whether 6 is asleep or out of range, and why it was then removed, is not known.

## Telling one actor from another on two games

| Kind | Id on the wire | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Placed (enemies, pots, chests, switches) | the u64 placement hash | Both games load the same Banc placement, and the hash is the key the flag store already uses ([flags.md](flags.md)) |
| Made by a player's game (projectiles, an enemy a player's action spawned) | the maker's player number and a u32 counter of its own | Nothing else names it on both sides; the maker says "I made 7" and everyone calls it (2, 7) |
| Made by an enemy (bullets, summons) | the same, made by the enemy's owner | The enemy's owner is the only game whose copy really fires |
| Made by the scene with no hash (spawners, waves; the night Stalchild now go by "Rolled spawns" below) | kind placed, value = `SceneSpawnId(scene, actor name, order)`: FNV-1a 64 of the three with the top bit set. `order` counts the enemies of that name seen in this scene visit, first seen first (one walk ordered by x, then z) | Stalchild were seen with hash ~0 (Caleb's test, 2026-11-09). The n-th Stalchild in game A and the n-th in game B get the same id, so a hit on one shows on the other, even when each game spawned its own near its own Link. Positions are not shared yet, so the two may stand in different places; a later rule can make the scene host's game the only spawner and hide the others' extras |

A placed id is only good inside one scene, since a scene can be loaded on one game and not the other. Messages that name a placed object say its scene once, in `OBJECT_PRESENT`.

**The rule** (branch `objects-6`, `coop_objects_sync.cpp`, tested in `objects_test.cpp`):

- Each game knows the others' scenes from their `PLAYER_SAMPLE`s (a scene older than 5 s does not count). The **decider** of a scene is the lowest player number in it. Only the decider's rolls are kept.
- **Far apart, each rolls its own** (issue 124, branch `boss-targets`; `Sync::RollerFor`, tested in `objects_test.cpp`). The game spawns Stalchild around its own Link, so with one decider for the whole field a player far from player 1 got none. Now a roll is kept by the lowest player number whose Link (its last `PLAYER_SAMPLE` position, under 5 s old) is within 40 m of where the roll rose (`kRollNearM`); a player in the scene whose place is unknown or old counts as near (the rule before). With no lower-numbered player that near, a game keeps and shares its own roll as a made id with itself as maker, and the others make copies of it as of the decider's. The `rolls decided by player N` line still names the lowest number in the scene.
- On the decider, a new hashless `Enemy_Stalchild` or `Enemy_Stalchild_Big` gets a made id (maker = the decider, its own counter) and is sent once as `OBJECT_SPAWN` (name, place, rotation, animation, life); then samples as for any enemy it owns, and `OBJECT_REMOVE` (dead or gone) when it leaves the list.
- On every other game in the scene, its own roll is deleted on the next walk with the game's delete request (0xa6490), the same way as a taken pickup, and counted. The spawner is not stopped, so it may roll again and be deleted again: the log line `rolls decided by player N, C copies to make, R own rolls deleted` shows how often.
- The decider's `OBJECT_SPAWN` makes the spawn director (`CoopSpawn::Director::SetEnemies`, keys with the top bit set, `place` off) create the same actor by name at the decider's spot. When it is on the list it takes the decided id; from then on the Mover places it where the decider's one is and plays the decider's animation at its frame (so the rise from the ground is the decider's rise). A fresh Stalchild within 4 m of a copy still being made is not deleted for 3 s, since it is likely that copy before the director lists it. At the decider's `OBJECT_REMOVE` the director deletes the copy.
- The director needs the svc cave (`LICHII_COOP_PUPPET` or `LICHII_COOP_SPAWN`), as the Mover does.
- **A copy that sinks by itself is made again** (issue 124). In the chunk 2 test (`stalchild` scenario, 2026-11-09) B made A's three Stalchild at A's spots within 0.1 s, but each copy played its own `Disappear` 2 s later and left B's list about 4 s after it was made, while A's fought on for 10 more seconds. So B showed no Stalchild during the fight, and A's kills had nothing to reach in B. B's clock was already night (set to 20:00 4 s before) and B stood about as far from them as A's Link, so the trigger is something else in the Stalchild's battle AI (`Enemy_Stalchild_LandBattle.actionseq` reads a create-time range, a return distance and the user's perception; the `Disappear` module ends in `OneShotGenericDeleteThisActor`). Not traced. A later roll copied at 135 s stayed and attacked. Now: when a decided copy leaves our walk while the decider's one is still there (no `OBJECT_REMOVE` yet), the director makes it again 1 s later, at most 3 times (`CoopSpawn::Copy::remakes`, `Sync::kRemakes`), and the new one gets the 3 s create wait again so it is not taken for an own roll. A copy that left dead (life 0 or a death animation, from a hit here) is not made again. Log: `coop: spawn: Enemy_Stalchild: copy <key> went by itself; made again (n of 3)`, or `not made again` after the third. A copy waiting to be made again is not made if by then the input says remakes 0 (it died here: the director sees it go at once, Sync knows it died two walks later).
- **This is a stop-gap.** The copy's own AI still runs: the remade copy has the same reason to sink and may do it again about 2 s later, so 3 remakes cover roughly 10 s of a fight. The real fix is one of: (a) hold the copies' AI (`LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_AI_HOLD`, "Holding a copy's AI", still off and unproven, and it holds every copy, not only Stalchild), or (b) find what in `Enemy_Stalchild_LandBattle.actionseq` sends a Stalchild to `Disappear` (its create-time range or the user-perception check) and keep that false for copies. Many `not made again` lines in a game test mean it is time for (a) or (b).
- Not done: a decider who leaves the scene (its made ones are then gone for the server too, and the next player starts rolling); the copy's rise starts when `OBJECT_SPAWN` arrives, so it is about one round trip later than the decider's (the Mover then sets it to the decider's frame).

### Hatched enemies (Gohma's larvae, issue 178)

Issue 178, a player test in the Deku Tree: Gohma's eggs killed as eggs were shared, but once one hatched it stayed an egg on one screen and attacked as a larva on the other.

**Why.** An egg and its larva are two actors (`Enemy_Gohma_Egg` or `Enemy_DungeonBoss_Gohma_Egg`, then `Enemy_Gohma_Larva`; each has its own `AS` file). Neither has a placement hash in the Deku Tree (the scene places one `Enemy_Gohma_Egg`, no larva; Gohma drops the rest), so both go by `SceneSpawnId`. Only the egg's owner runs it; every other game's copy is held in the owner's animation and cannot hatch. So the egg went (alive) and a larva came in the owner's game only: no game had a rule to end the egg copy or make the larva elsewhere.

**Now** (`IsHatchingEgg`, `IsHatchling`, `Sync::Hatch`; tested in `objects_test.cpp`, gohma-larva cases):
- A fresh `Enemy_Gohma_Larva` within 3 m of an egg this game owns (still on the walk, or gone alive at most 2 s before) came out of it. It gets a made id (maker = this game) and is sent once as `OBJECT_SPAWN`, as a rolled enemy is; then samples and, when it leaves, `OBJECT_REMOVE` dead or gone. Log: `share hatched here: Enemy_Gohma_Larva`.
- The egg is ended with `OBJECT_REMOVE` reason gone (with its epoch) once both have happened. An egg of ours that left alive stays on our `OBJECT_PRESENT` list for those 2 s, since the server hands an unlisted object to another game at once and would then refuse the remove. This also holds back the hand-over of an egg that left our walk alive without hatching (we walked away, the room unloaded) by those 2 s. An egg that left with life 0 or in a death animation is a kill, not a hatch (the kill rules above).
- Other games: `OBJECT_REMOVE` gone for a placed egg (epoch not older than they know) deletes their copy at once (`share hatched in its owner's game: <egg>`), and the made `OBJECT_SPAWN` of a larva makes the spawn director create one where the owner's is (as for rolled spawns). A fresh larva within 4 m of a copy still being made waits for it, so it is not taken for a larva of their own.
- A larva next to an egg another game owns (its copy hatched by itself) keeps the old placed id: not shared as made. A larva with no egg of ours near is the same.
- Not covered: a late entrant whose game still lists the scene's placed egg after it hatched elsewhere. The server keeps a removed placed object owned by nobody until no game lists it, and nothing tells the newcomer that it hatched (a "gone" has no late-entrant record, unlike a kill), so that egg may stand with no owner.
- Two eggs within 3 m of each other: a larva that appears while its egg is still on the walk is matched to the nearest egg of ours, which may be its sibling (review of gohma-larva). Since branch gohma-sync (issue 178): when an egg of ours that was not matched leaves alive while a sibling within 6 m is matched but still on the walk, the leaving egg is the one that hatched: it is ended (`OBJECT_REMOVE` gone, log `hatched here (its sibling egg was matched first)`) and the sibling is unmatched, free for its own larva. Tested in `objects_test.cpp` (gohma-sync case).
- Deku Tree, other enemies with a stage change, checked: Gohma herself and the Deku Babas (`Enemy_Dekubaba`, buried, then up) change stage by animation on one actor, which the Mover copies, and the wake rule hands the enemy to the game whose Link woke it; no gap. The big Skulltula drops by animation too. Outside the Deku Tree the same gap may exist for an enemy that makes another actor at run time (names from the game's `Pack/Actor` list): the Peahat's larvae (`Enemy_Peahat_Larva`, summoned by `Enemy_Peahat_AircraftCarrier`), King Dodongo's babies (`Enemy_DungeonBoss_Dodongo_SpawnBabyDodongo`, `Enemy_BabyDodongo`) and the bubbles of Jabu-Jabu's big Octo (`Enemy_Shabom_BigOctoChild`). Not checked in a game; add each to `IsHatchingEgg`/`IsHatchling` (or a summoner list) once its names are confirmed in a game.
- Not proven: that the director can make `Enemy_Gohma_Larva` in the other game (its pack must be loaded there; the egg's is, so likely), and that the larva comes out within 3 m of the egg's last place.

## Design

### Projectiles

- **The maker owns it, for its whole life.** No hand-over: an arrow lives under a second, a bomb a few. This is the TotK lesson in reverse: there, arrows and bombs were never synced and only their damage was, which showed nothing on the other screen. Here both are done.
- **Copies are harmless.** On other screens a projectile is drawn with a small pack of our own (`CoopShot_Arrow`, `CoopShot_Seed`, `CoopShot_Bomb`, `CoopShot_Bombchu`, `CoopShot_Nut`, `CoopShot_Boomerang`, `CoopShot_Hook`), built like the puppet's: the game's own model, animation and effect files by path, no physics, sensor, chemical, attack or AI. A copy can never hit, explode, light a torch or pull Link. So nothing it does needs to be undone, and no hit is counted twice.
- **What the maker sends.** `OBJECT_SPAWN` once, reliable, with the actor kind, position, velocity, rotation and its server time; then `OBJECT_SAMPLE` while it flies, for the ones that do not fly straight (bomb rolling or held, bombchu, boomerang, hook tip), at up to 30 a second; then `OBJECT_REMOVE` with the reason and the place (hit, exploded, gone). An arrow and a seed need no samples: the copy flies them from the spawn's position and velocity, with gravity (to measure: the arrow's physics param), until the remove.
- **Explosions and hits shown.** On `OBJECT_REMOVE` with reason exploded, the copy plays the bomb's explosion effect and sound at that place and disappears; with reason hit, an arrow copy sticks there for a moment and a seed or arrow gets its hit spark and sound there (impact actors, [spawns.md](spawns.md#where-a-shot-lands-issue-166)). The effect's name comes from `Item_Bomb`'s `ELink` and `SLink` files (not read).
- **Held bombs (issue 169).** See [spawns.md](spawns.md), "Held bombs and the fuse".
- **What projectiles do to the world.** A bombed wall, a shot eye switch, a lit torch: what is saved goes through shared flags ([protocol.md](protocol.md), stage 4). What is not saved (a timed switch, a torch) is not shared at first and is listed under "Not done".

#### What is built (branch `projectiles`, issue 85)

The copies are the game's own actors made by the spawn sync ([spawns.md](spawns.md), `LICHII_COOP_SPAWN=1`), except seeds, arrows, hookshot tips and boomerangs. Since issue 85 those are the `CoopShot_Seed`, `CoopShot_Arrow`, `CoopShot_Hook`, `CoopShot_HookLong` and `CoopShot_Boomerang` stand-ins ([spawns.md](spawns.md), "Seeds, arrows and hookshot tips"); the other `CoopShot_*` packs above are not made. On top of it, `lichii/hook/coop_projectiles.{h,cpp}` (tested in `tests/lichii/projectiles_test.cpp`) holds the rules below, used by `coop_spawn.cpp` and `coop_objects_sync.cpp`. No new message: everything rides on `OBJECT_SPAWN`, `OBJECT_SAMPLE` and `OBJECT_REMOVE` components that protocol 4 already has (rotation, fuse), so the protocol version stays.

| Kind | Actor | Copy's path | Its end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bomb | `Item_Bomb` | samples (held, thrown, rolling) | blown up at the owner's fuse; `exploded` if it lived 1 s |
| Bombchu | `Item_Bombchu` | samples | blown up at the owner's remove |
| Deku nut | `Item_DekuNut` | samples, carried on along its arc | deleted; `hit` if it went at 3 m/s or more |
| Slingshot seed | `Shootable_DekuSeed` | the same; a bounce is sent at once | the same; a `hit` copy stays 0.2 s where it struck ([spawns.md](spawns.md), issue 166) |
| Arrow (all elements) | `NormalArrow` | the same | the same; a `hit` copy sticks 1.5 s where it struck |
| Boomerang | `Weapon_Boomerang`, shown as `CoopShot_Boomerang` (the game's own one would home on this game's enemies) | samples (it homes and turns back) | deleted |
| Hookshot, longshot tip | `Shootable_Hook`, `Shootable_HookLong` | samples, with the tip's animation (its model is the chain, `Item_Hookshot_Chain`; the chain's length is its animation set's `ChainExtend`) | deleted |

- **Turned the owner's way.** The maker reads the 3x3 at actor +0x328 with the place and sends it as a quaternion in the spawn and every sample. The copy is created with that turn in the create argument, and each game frame the director writes place and turn as one 48-byte block at +0x31c, then calls the actor's own vtable slot 0x218 `(actor, 0)` (0x4c6580 on every actor seen; the director checks the slot points into main), as `ExecuteActorForceSetMtx` does, so the physics takes the new place. `LICHII_COOP_SHOTS_SETTER=0` leaves the call out.
- **The same path at the same time.** A copy is placed where the owner's projectile is *now*: the latest sample carried on by its velocity for the time since the sample's server time (at most 250 ms), with the fall that two samples show (velocity y lost per second, 0 to 60 m/s²) for arrows, seeds and nuts. Before, it was carried on by the time since the message arrived, so a copy ran one network delay behind.
- **Smooth flight** (branch `smooth-shots`). In a hand test other players' shots looked choppy: the copy was carried on from the latest sample by the velocity the owner measured between two walks, and that swings (0 or twice the speed when the owner draws 60 frames a second and its game moves the shot 30 times), so the copy stood still or jumped and snapped at each sample. Now a copy is shown 100 ms late on the server clock, as late as the puppet that shot it, between the two samples around that time (`Track::Shown`), so it moves the same each frame and leaves the puppet's hands, not ahead of them. Only when no sample is that recent is it carried on as before (with the fall, at most 250 ms). A hit goes on to where the owner's shot struck (taken a walk after its last sample) and stays there; every copy the owner removed (hit or gone) is kept 100 ms longer, so it is seen reaching the hit spot or the hand before its land hold starts (a nut flashes at the wall, a boomerang comes back to the hand); a bomb that goes off does so at once where the owner's did. A thrown bomb no longer jumps 100 ms ahead when it leaves the puppet's hands.
- **Explosions at the same moment.** The maker learns its game's bomb fuse from its own bombs (the middle of the last 7 that lived 1 to 10 s; log `coop: shots: our bombs' fuse is ...`). From then on its bombs' spawn and samples carry `fuse` (seconds left). Another game blows its copy up when the server clock reaches the sample's time plus that fuse, without waiting for the remove (`coop: shots: bomb copy blown on the owner's fuse`); the remove still blows it up if it comes first (a bomb hit by fire), and the first bomb of a session, with no fuse known yet, goes off at the remove as before.
- **No hit taken twice.** A copy bomb is a real bomb in its game, so it hurts that game's enemies too. Each copy that goes off (by the owner's fuse, its remove, or by itself) is noted with its place and time. The objects sync then treats a life drop within 6 m of it, from 0.2 s before to 1.2 s after, as that blast's: on a copy it is not sent as `DAMAGE` (the owner's own bomb deals it there), and on an enemy we own it is kept as credit for 2 s, which a `DAMAGE` from the thrower's game for the same bomb uses up first (log `coop: shots: life drop by a projectile copy's blast kept back`). When the `DAMAGE` comes first (no fuse known yet, so the copy goes off at the remove, one network delay late: each player's first bomb of a session, and every bombchu), it is kept as debit for 2 s and the copy's blast drop after it is written back up by that much. A copy bomb removed as `gone` (under 1 s) is no blast.
- **Why it went.** `OBJECT_REMOVE` says `exploded` for a bomb or bombchu that lived 1 s, `hit` for a nut, seed or arrow that left the list at 3 m/s or more (it struck something), else `gone`.
- **Arrow elements.** Fire, ice and light arrows are all `NormalArrow` (inferred). The shooter's element is already known to every game from its `PLAYER_EQUIP` arrow; `ElementOf` and `CauseOf` turn it into a damage cause. The copy is a plain arrow: how the game gives an arrow its element was not found.

Not proven: nothing of this has run in a game. The credit and debit are per enemy, not per thrower: on the owner, its own bomb beside another player's copy blast within 2 s of a `DAMAGE` can cancel against it (one hit lost or put back). Copy arrows and seeds that hit an enemy copy are not guarded (blasts only). Thrown from the puppet: the copy starts where the owner's projectile was made (the owner's hand), and the puppet stands at the owner's place 100 ms late, so the two should meet; not seen.

#### Enemy shots (branch `enemy-shots`)

Each game runs the AI of its copy of a shared enemy, and a copy plays the owner's animations, so every game's Octorok spat its own rock (aimed at its own Link) and none was the other's. Now the game that owns the enemy fires, for everyone. `lichii/hook/coop_enemy_shots.{h,cpp}` (tested in `tests/lichii/enemy_shots_test.cpp`) holds the rules; the spawn sync carries the shots like a player's projectiles (no new message, no protocol change). `LICHII_COOP_ENEMY_SHOTS=0` turns it off (each game its own shots, as before).

- **Which names.** From the game's `Pack/Actor`: `Octorok_Bullet`, `Dodongo_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossDodongo_Bullet_*`, `ScrubDeku_DekuNut`, `ScrubForestStage_DekuNut`, `SkullKid_Bullet`, `PoeHyrule_Bullet_*`, `FlareDancer_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossGanondrof_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossPhantomGanon_Bullet_*`, `PhantomGanon_Bullet_*`, `Twinrova_Bullet_*` and `KotakeKoumeTwinrova_Bullet_*` (not the `_MirrorShieldReflect` ones: Link's), `Ganon_Bullet_*`, `Ganondorf_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossGanon_Bullet_*`, `Enemy_MoblinClub_ShockWave` (taken out of the objects sync's enemies). Each has its maker's name (`Enemy_Octorok`, `Enemy_Scrub_`, `Enemy_DungeonBoss_Dodongo`, ...). Beams, breath and auras (`BeamosBeam`, `Enemy_DungeonBoss_Barinade_Beam`, `DungeonBossVolvagia_FireBreath`, `Bubble_Green_Bullet_Aura`) are not shots and stay each game's own. Only `Octorok_Bullet` and the scrub nut are in the brief's test; the others are names only.
- **Whose shot.** After each of its walks the objects sync notes every shared enemy with its place and owner (ours, another player's, not known). When the spawn sync first sees a new enemy shot, the nearest enemy of its maker's name within 10 m (30 m for a boss or mini-boss) decides: ours, the shot is sent (`OBJECT_SPAWN` with the projectile flag, samples at up to 30 a second, `OBJECT_REMOVE`); another player's, it is a copy enemy's shot and is deleted; no maker found or no owner known yet, it is left alone and not sent. Log: `coop: shots: enemy shot Octorok_Bullet at x y z: sent (our enemy's)` / `deleted (a copy's; the owner's comes)` / `kept here (no owner known)`.
- **The copy hits.** The other games make the owner's shot as the game's own actor (no stand-in), placed on the owner's path every frame with the setter, so it can hit their Link where it meets him: the player it was aimed at takes the hit in their own game, once. In the owner's game the real shot flies through that player's puppet (a puppet has no body to hit).
- **The delete.** A copy enemy's own shot is given to the director after it was seen on 2 walks (so a copy the director has just made, not yet reported, is never taken for one; a shot is also never claimed as a copy by its place). On the calc thread, just before the game's delete request, it is checked again: still on the actor list (its node's owner is itself), the same vtable as when seen, none of the director's copies, and no create of ours waiting to be taken; each address once. `LICHII_COOP_ENEMY_SHOTS_HIDE=0` keeps them (shared, but doubled), to compare. Log: `coop: enemy shot 0x... of a copy enemy deleted (the owner's is shown)`.

Not proven: nothing of this has run in a game. Whether a shot made by our create request without its enemy flies and hurts (it might wait for its maker, or delete itself; then the copy is gone and the hit is not taken here), whether deleting a copy enemy's shot leaves that enemy waiting on it, and whether the copy's own AI fires on the same frame as the owner's (the delete then comes 2 walks later: the shot can be seen for about 50 ms).

### Enemies

The rules answer each way the TotK sync broke ([protocol.md](protocol.md) "Rules it is built on"; the TotK port's `docs/online-sync.md`, section 3).

- **Who may own.** Only a game that has the enemy loaded and awake. Each game says what it has in `OBJECT_PRESENT`.
- **First owner.** The first game to report the enemy present. Two at once: whichever list reaches the server first; if the other player is the one near it, the distance rule below moves it 3 s later.
- **Fight lock.** An enemy is "in a fight" from the first change of its life, or from its owner's report that it is targeting a player, until 12 s after the last of those (60 s for a boss). In a fight it keeps its owner. It moves only when the owner leaves the session or the scene, unloads it, is loading, has been paused over 6 s, or has been dead over 10 s; or by the hit rule below.
- **Out of a fight.** It moves to another player only if that player is within 30 m of it and at least 10 m nearer than the owner, and either the owner is over 40 m away or that player is within 20 m, for 3 s in a row, and not within 5 s of the last change. The 20 m part was added for issue 101: an enemy's AI chases only its own game's Link, so the game of the chased player is the only one that moves it right. In that run the owner stood 34 m from two Peahat larvae that chased the other player, and "owner over 40 m" alone would have left them with it.
- **A chase takes it** (issue 101, branch `enemy-chase`). The distances above are to the owner's enemy, which stays home while another game's copy chases that game's Link far away; so no distance ever saw the chased player near it. Each game now tells the server which copies chase its Link (`OBJECT_PRESENT` bit 2, targeting), and out of a fight, not within 5 s of the last change, the object goes to the game that has said so for 500 ms (the longest chase first, then the lower player number), wherever the owner's enemy stands. A chase on a group's part moves the root (and the part with it). The owner flagging its own enemy as targeting is a fight, so an enemy that chases its owner's Link stays: when both games' enemies chase their own Link nothing moves.
- **A hit can take it.** A `DAMAGE` from a player who is within 30 m of the enemy (of the owner's enemy, or of where the hit landed: the hitter's copy, which may have chased the hitter far from the owner's enemy, issue 101) and at least 10 m nearer than its owner moves it to that player at once (not within 5 s of the last change), fight or not: the enemy fights whoever hits it. Two players in the same fight are never 10 m apart in their distance to it, so their hits do not flip it, which is what the TotK sync got wrong. The hit itself still goes to the old owner first, and the life the new owner gets is never above the life before the hit minus the hit, so a hit that crossed the hand-over is not lost.
- **Groups.** A boss and its parts, and an enemy and what it fired or summoned, are one group with one owner. A group's root is the boss (or the summoner); parts follow it. Part lists come from the actor names (`DungeonBossDodongo_Bullet_*` follows `DungeonBossDodongo`, and so on), checked for each boss when its fight is tried. The game that knows the names tells the server with `OBJECT_GROUP` (member, root, a boss flag); the server holds no names. A member is never moved alone: it follows its root's owner, and a sign of a fight on any member is the root's fight. A projectile stays with its maker until it is gone, whatever its group.
- **Hand-over carries the state.** The server asks the owner to let go; the owner answers with its last state (position, velocity, rotation, life, animation command and frame); the server passes it to the new owner and only then announces the change. If the owner is gone or has not answered within 500 ms, the server uses the last sample it relayed. If the player it was for has left or unloaded it meanwhile, nothing changes. The new owner places the enemy at that state advanced by the velocity over its age, and sets its life to the lower of its own and the received life, so a boss never heals across a hand-over.
- **An owner epoch.** Each change of owner adds 1 to the object's epoch. Every sample and damage event carries it, and a receiver drops anything from an older epoch. A late sample from the old owner can then never pull the enemy back. One thing from the epoch before is still taken, for 2 s: the old owner's `OBJECT_REMOVE` with reason dead, since its enemy really died before it heard of the change; it is passed on with the new epoch, the new owner included.
- **Copies.** A non-owner's enemy is the game's own actor (it is placed, so it exists anyway). Each frame the hook writes the owner's interpolated pose into it, as for the puppet, and its life from the latest sample (never above it). Its AI still runs; holding it still is a later step (TotK's `TOTK_SYNC_AI_HOLD` idea), measured first.
- **Only the owner removes.** A copy whose life reaches 0 here is held at 1 until the owner's sample says 0, so the enemy dies once, on every screen, when its owner says so. The owner's `OBJECT_REMOVE` (reason dead) removes copies. A non-owner unloading its copy only sends `OBJECT_PRESENT` without it.

### Damage

One path for every kind of hit, as TotK's `TOTK_SYNC_DAMAGE` found worked best (`docs/online-sync-ws3.md` there): read the life of every enemy copy right after the hook writes it, and again at the next frame hook, after the game's own frame. A drop is a hit taken on this screen.

- Drops on one copy are summed for 100 ms from the first, then sent once as `DAMAGE` to the owner, with the amount, the cause if known and where it hit.
- Not sent: when the owner's own life for it came down by the same amount since 50 ms before (both games ran the same hit), when our player is over 80 m away, or over 20 a second.
- The owner takes the amount off its enemy (life at least 0) and sends a sample at once. Its life change starts the fight lock, but does not move the enemy (above).
- Hits that change no life (a Deku nut's stun, a boomerang's stun, a hookshot pull) are not seen by a life read. They need the cause sent instead: `DAMAGE` with amount 0 and the cause, applied by whatever the game does for that cause. Not found yet.
- Projectiles hit only on their maker's screen, so a remote player's arrow reaches a copy on the maker's game, and that game forwards the drop. Copies of projectiles never hit.
- Players are not hurt by each other, and puppets have no life. Enemy attacks on a puppet do nothing; an enemy attacks the player of its owner's game. Whether enemies should target puppets is a later question.

## The hook side (first cut)

`LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS=1` turns it on (off by default). `lichii/hook/coop_objects_sync.cpp` holds the rules, tested in `tests/lichii/objects_test.cpp`; `coop_objects.cpp` walks the actor list every second frame and writes lives. Only actors whose name starts with `Enemy_` are shared.

- `OBJECT_PRESENT` lists the enemies loaded in our scene (the scene of `PLAYER_SAMPLE`), on every change and every 2 s. A new scene forgets every id.
- The owner sends `OBJECT_SAMPLE` (time, position, life) at most 20 times a second while life or place changes, once a second while still.
- On a game that does not own an enemy, a drop of its life is our hit: the drops over 100 ms go to the owner as one `DAMAGE` (cause unknown). The owner writes life minus the amount (not below 0) into its enemy, and its next sample carries it.
- A copy takes the owner's life from samples: a lower life at once, a higher one only when none of our hits is in flight (500 ms after our `DAMAGE`), so it does not heal back between our hit and the owner's answer. `HANDOVER_STATE` gives a new owner the last life.
- Animals are shared the same way (`Animal_` names, so a cucco's hits and place are shared), except the ridable Epona (`Animal_Epona_Adult`, `CoopRide::kHorse`): each game keeps its own, and the ride sync shows the other player's horse under its puppet (epona-ride; shared, one game's Epona was a frozen copy under its rider, or the other game drew two horses).
- **Copies follow the owner** (branch `objects-4`). The owner's samples also carry rotation (the 3x3 matrix at +0x328 as a quaternion), velocity (from the change of place between walks) and the animation (AS slot 0: command, frame, rate; a new command alone is worth a sample). Every other game works out, each presented frame, the owner's pose 100 ms in the past, blended between the two samples around that time or carried on by the velocity for at most 150 ms past the last. A copy with no sample for 2.5 s is left to itself; an enemy we own is never moved. `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_POSE=0` turns this off.
- **Moving an actor needs the game's own setter.** A plain write of +0x31c from the emulator is undone within a frame (two-game test, 2026-11-09: copies were 4.8 m off on average, up to 71 m). The AI node `ExecuteActorForceSetMtx` (run 0x2622e50) writes +0x31c..+0x34b and then calls the actor's vtable slot 0x218 with `(actor, 0)`. Every actor seen has the vtable 0x4b25e18, whose slot 0x218 is **0x4c6580**: it sets bit 23 of actor +0x4a8 and hands the new transform to the physics component (table +0x50, 0x4c6630 and 0x4c6870). So the hook's Mover, a caller of the svc cave (`CoopSvc::AddCaller`, order 30, after the puppets, spawn copies and fairies), does the same on the game's calc thread each game frame for every copy, after checking that the actor is still on the list (its node points back at it), awake (state 4) and has that vtable. Live: copies held exactly where they were put (0.000 m off over 1,488 moves in 5 s). The cave is patched only when `LICHII_COOP_PUPPET` or `LICHII_COOP_SPAWN` is on.
- **Animations and hit reactions.** On copies the Mover plays the owner's AS slot 0 command through the game's play request (0x2022a0, as for the puppet) and checks the copy every game frame on the calc thread: when its slot 0 shows another name (the copy's own AI took over), the owner's one is played again at once and started at the owner's frame (0x37b5d70 `(controller, 0, s0 = frame)`, as the puppet), the sample's frame plus rate x 30 a second, shown 100 ms late like the pose; on each new sample a copy more than 3 frames off is set to it. Before (Caleb's hand test, 2026-11-09) the replay came every 250 ms from frame 0, so a cucco near the other player and a carried cucco looked frozen in a loop (`Lifted` replayed 42 times in 11 s). Not yet seen live with this change. The names live in one block from the game's malloc (0x14c6c90), one fixed cell per name, since a slot may keep the pointer. Live: a ReDead hit by A's sword showed the same place and pose in B, and B's copy played `Standing`, `Turn`, `Run`, `Wait`, `Attack_Start`, `Attack_Fail` right after A's. The red hit flash is not an animation and is not copied.
- **Life on copies is written directly.** The Life step 0x49d8c0 reads an s32 at Life + 0x228 (and Life + 0x1350), stores their sum at Life + 0x1cb0 and takes it off life; but Life + 0x228 belongs to a whole hit record (+0x1f0..+0x2f0: a present byte at +0x24c, a hit id at +0x248 compared with the last at +0x1b18, the attacker at +0x240..+0x26c, copied to +0x1b20.. as the last hit). An amount written there alone was never taken (live). `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_HURT=1` still tries it, with a direct write after 400 ms; off by default. 0xaa920, which clears Life + 0x228, is the Life reset, not a per-frame step. Hit records seen live (A's ReDead, Kokiri Sword): +0x1f8 `00060001 40040000`, +0x200 the hit id (0xbc, 0xbe, 0xc0), +0x218 the hit actor itself.
- **One-time pickups taken elsewhere.** A key64 flag arriving set to 1 (`FLAG_SET`, `FLAG_STATE` or the snapshot) marks that placement key taken; a loaded actor with that placement hash (a Gold Skulltula, its `Obj_RupeeToken`) is no longer shared and is deleted once with the game's delete request (0xa6490 `(actor, 0)`) by the Mover. Live with a test key (the ReDead's hash): B deleted its ReDead 0.4 s after, A kept its own. Not yet run with a real Skulltula.
- Test switches (two-game tests only): `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_TEST_DRIVE=1` holds one of player 1's owned enemies 3 m ahead of Link, swaying, hurt by 1 every 4 s; `=2` holds it still 2 m ahead for real sword hits; `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_TEST_TAKE=<hex key>` acts on player 2's game as if that key arrived taken, 3 s after sharing starts.
- **Order-puzzle actors are each game's own** (issue 149, `CoopObjects::IsOwnPuzzle`): `Enemy_Scrub_Deku_Brothers` and `Enemy_Scrub_ForestStage` are not shared, because their order is counted in each game's manager from its own hits (see [dungeon.md](dungeon.md), "Order puzzles"). Nor (issue 151) is the fishing pond's owner (`Npc_Hylia_Fishman*`): he takes each player's fee and judges the catch in that game's own events, and his animation on a copy spoiled this player's try ([items.md](items.md), "The fishing pond").
- **Fish and lures** (issue 151, fishing-2, fishing-3): fish (`Animal_Fish_*`, `CoopObjects::IsFish`) are shared animals, and the owner game is the one whose lure is nearest the fish. `Sync::SetLures` gets our own cast lures each walk (the spawn sync's `OwnLures`, through `CoopProjectiles::TheLures`). Each walk ends with `FISH_LURE` ([protocol.md](protocol.md), "Fish and lures"): every fish we have loaded, how far our nearest lure is from it (25 cm steps, none past 6 m), *hooked* when we own it and it is within 1 m of our lure, *drawn* when we own it and it comes at our lure at 0.2 m/s, or when it is a copy whose own AI swims it 5 mm a frame or more towards our lure (or holds within 1 m: a bite) for 1 s (no gap over 400 ms). The server gives each fish to the game whose lure beats the owner's (1 m nearer, or drawn when the owner's is not and not over 1 m farther; held 300 ms, not within 1 s of the last change) and keeps a hooked fish with its owner, even while that game is paused or a list is late (10 s). Only the owner's hooked counts. A copy's AI may turn at a far lure while the owner's fish circles a near one, so drawn alone never takes a fish from a nearer lure (the review of fishing-3: otherwise the fish went back and forth every 1.3 s). So the fish is ours before it reaches our lure, and our game runs the approach, the bite and the fight. A fish we own that leaves the walk within 3 m of our Link was landed: `OBJECT_REMOVE` dead, and the other games delete their copy at once (no life to lower). Other players' lures are model-only stand-ins ([spawns.md](spawns.md)), so no fish here goes for them.
  - *Why fishing-2 was not enough* (read from the code; fishing-2 never ran past the pond's door in a test). It moved fish with the enemies' rules: a copy that swam at our lure was flagged targeting (the chase rule), and the owner's fish at its own lure was flagged too, which the server takes as a fight. (1) That fight lock lasts 12 s after the last flag, so a fish that had been near A's lure could not go to B for 12 s, while B's copy bit B's lure and was pulled back each frame. (2) Out of a fight the distance rule gave a fish to whichever player stood 10 m nearer to it, wherever the lures were, so a fish swimming at A's lure could go to B. (3) The hand-over waited for the copy's own AI to swim at our lure for a second, which a fish that circles before it bites may never do. Now a fish is never flagged targeting or fighting, the server moves fish only by the lures, and the nearest lure wins before the fish starts its approach.
  - Left: a copy whose AI bites our lure while the owner's lure is nearer still bites a fish the Mover pulls back (both lures within a metre or two of one fish). A copy landed in a game that does not own it is not removed elsewhere (the server takes a remove from the owner only). The thresholds (6 m, 1 m, 0.2 m/s, 5 mm a frame) are not measured.
- **Townspeople** (branch `objects-5`): `Npc_` actors are shared like enemies and animals (place, rotation, animation, life), except each player's own: `Npc_Navi` (the fairy sync has her), `Npc_Equipment_*` (held equipment) and `Npc_Invisible_*` helpers, which the first Kakariko run showed being shared. Live: both games in `KakarikoVillage_Child`, A sharing 7 townspeople and animals, B holding 5 copies where A's are (0.000 m off). No townsperson was in either camera.

- **Holding a copy's AI** (same branch; read from the code, nothing run). Two ways the game itself stops an actor's AI were found:
  - *Pause.* The AI node `OneShotRequestPauseOwn` (run 0x291e980) calls **0xa6740** `(actor, type)`, beside the delete request 0xa6490: it sets bit `type + 10` of the u32 at actor +0x30 (six pause kinds, bits 10 to 15) and the actor goes to state 5. A paused actor does not run at all, so its animation would stand still too; the Mover also only moves actors in state 4. Not used.
  - *Brain skip.* The BSA component (the enemy's "brain": which verb it is in, such as idle, search, battle) is component-table entry **+0xb0** (0xcad060). `QueryBsaIsBrainSkip` (run 0x29ce250) reads bit 0 of its u16 at **+0xc4**. A loop over all BSA components (around 0x369b7c to 0x36a070) rewrites that word every frame: bits 0 to 3 come from a virtual call on the object at manager +0x950 (slot 0x40 or 0x48, at 0x369df8), the rest from the manager's own state (+0x930, +0x9f0) and the component's u64 at +0xb8. So a bit written from outside is likely replaced before the brain reads it.
  - Who reads the bit: 0xcc010 and 0xd89a4 load the actor's BSA component and test bit 0 of +0xc4 (`tbz`, the clear case taking the same path as an actor with no BSA component). Which way it works (set = this frame's brain is skipped, as the query's name says, or set = it runs) could not be told from the code alone.
  - **The hold** (`LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_AI_HOLD`, off by default; `coop_hold.h`, `coop_hold.s`, patched in by `coop_svc.cpp` beside the co-op cave). The call at **main+0x369df8** (`blr x8`, the one whose answer gives bits 0 to 3) becomes `bl 0x3c46300`, a 76-byte cave that makes the same call and then, when the component in `x21` is in a table at main+0x3c46400, changes the answer to `(bits & and) | or`. The table (count, and, or, up to 64 components) is written by the Mover at the start of every game frame from `Sync::Holds`: the `Enemy_` copies we do not own that the owner's samples place right now. An enemy that becomes ours, or whose owner fell silent for 2.5 s, is not in the next table, so its own AI runs again from that frame. Mode `=1` sets bit 0 on held copies, `=2` clears it.
  - What a run shows: the loader line `Applying "coop ai hold"`, and every 5 s `ai hold mode M: N copy-frames held, brain skip bit as asked in K; P plays in S s`. K equal to N proves the cave reaches the copies' components. If neither mode does it, the brain is gated elsewhere: next to read are the two readers above and the pause request.

### Groups

Enemies made of several actors share one owner and one fight (issue 105). `CoopObjects::GroupsOf` (`lichii/hook/coop_objects_sync.cpp`, tested in `tests/lichii/objects_test.cpp`) finds them in each walk by actor name; `Sync::Tick` sends `OBJECT_GROUP` right after `OBJECT_PRESENT` (so the server knows every member), on every change and again with every presence. The server does the rest (`Session::on_object_group`): a member follows its root's owner, and a fight on any member is the root's.

- A part follows the loaded actor of its root's name; with several roots of that name, the nearest within the rule's reach (lower id on a tie, so every game picks the same). A part keeps the root it was linked to while within 1.25 times the reach, so one near the edge does not flip between groups. A part this game linked whose root is gone or out of reach is sent once with itself as root, so it follows nobody; a part never linked here (a stray larva with no boss loaded) is not sent at all, so it gets no boss fight lock and cannot undo another game's link.
- A boss or mini-boss (`Enemy_DungeonBoss_*`, `Enemy_MiddleBoss_*`, the roots of boss groups) is sent with itself and the boss flag, alone too, for the 60 s fight lock. Picture book `_Dictionary` actors never count. Parts linked to a boss carry the flag too (one fight, one lock).
- Proof in a run: the hook's `share owner <id> is player P epoch E group G` line is logged on a change of the group too (enemy-sync-3; before, a part joining a group changed only G and was not logged, so no run could show a group), and the server prints `coop_server: enemies: group entries taken N, refused M; damage relayed ..., dropped ...; kills told late ...` at most every 5 s while the counts change. Parts the game spawns with no placement hash (Morpha's tentacles) get an id from their spawn order in the scene, so they match between games only while both spawned the same number.
- Names from the game's `Pack/Actor` list (2026-11-09). A boss's own parts are certain by name; what it summons (eggs, larvae, babies, a flock) is read from the names and only counts within 40 m. None of it has been seen in a fight yet.

**What counts as a drop.** An actor the game made (no placement hash) named `Obj_Rupee_Green`, `_Blue`, `_Red`, `_Purple`, `_Silver`, `_Gold`, `Obj_Heart`, `Obj_MagicJar_Small`, `_Large`, `Obj_ArrowBundle_Small`, `_Medium`, `_Large`, `Obj_Bomb`, `Obj_Bombchu`, `Obj_DekuNut`, `Obj_DekuSeed`, `Obj_DekuStick`, `Obj_Arrow_Fire`, `Obj_Fairy` (names from the actor packs; only `Obj_Rupee_Green` was seen live as a drop). A fairy from a pot, grass, a stone or an enemy is a drop like the rest since branch `breakables` (#102: one rule): it is the same fairy in every game, it heals only whoever takes it, and it is then gone for everyone; each game's copy flies its own way, so the first player to reach it in their own game gets it. The fountain's and other placed fairies have a hash and stay each game's own. Not heart pieces, containers or tokens (one-time pickups are flags, [items.md](items.md)).

**Placed loot (bushes and grass, #102).** Player logs from 2026-11-10 and 11-11 showed the loot of Kokiri Forest's bushes and grass as *placed* drops: `Obj_Heart`, `Obj_Rupee_Blue` and `Obj_Rupee_Green` with a placement hash, laid out again each time the area loads, with the same hash in every player's game and every session (for example 0x3e4fb463e9948383, a blue rupee; a row of seven hearts with seven hashes). Every game has its own, so before this each player could take it. Now such a drop is not rolled or announced (there is nothing to match: every game has it); only its pickup is shared. A placed drop that leaves the list for two walks with our Link within 2.5 m was picked up: after the 0.5 s hold (called off by a scene change or by our Link leaving the list) `DROP_TAKE` goes out with the placed id (kind placed, the hash). Every game in the scene deletes its own actor with that hash, at once or whenever it loads, for as long as it stays in the scene; a delete that does not come true is asked again after 3 s. A placed drop never blinks out, so one gone with Link further away was only unloaded. Running through a row of hearts takes several at once, so for placed drops that is not an unload. A player who comes into the scene is told the placed loot already taken (with the drops still lying, after its `DROP_ASK`). Leaving the scene forgets the takes: the game lays the loot out again, as it does for one player. `LICHII_COOP_DROPS_PLACED=1` makes placed drops rolls like the rest (the old switch); the log line is `is placed (hash ...) at X Y Z: pickup shared` (its place, for a scenario).

**How the other games' own random drop is replaced, and why.** The game's own roll is not stopped: where the game decides a drop was not found, and a patch there could not be tried while no game may run. Instead every roll is let happen and the actor list is watched, the way the rolled Stalchild are handled:

- A drop actor that is new on the list (not there at the scene's first walk, not a copy) is announced once with `DROP_SPAWN`: its own id (maker and counter), name, place and server time. The flag "owned" is set when something this game owns (an enemy, a pot, a crate: `CoopObjects::OwnedActors`) stood within 3 m of it in the last 5 s.
- Two announcements from different games within 3 m and 3 s of each other are the rolls for the same thing. One stays: the one flagged owned, then the one from the lower player number. Every game works this out from the announcements alone, so all end with the same drop whatever order the messages came in (tested for a third game both ways).
- A game whose own roll lost keeps that actor as the winner's when it is the same kind (nothing is seen to change), else deletes it with the game's delete request (0xa6490, through `CoopDrops::Remover`, svc caller 40, which first checks the address still holds a live actor of that name). A game with no actor for the winner makes one by name at the winner's place through the spawn director (`Director::SetDrops`, `Act::release`: once made, the director lets go of the slot and the drop lies by itself). It waits 0.5 s before making it, so its own roll can be kept instead.
- So a thing drops the same item at the same place everywhere. When the winner's game rolled nothing but another game rolled something, that one stays for everybody: a drop is a little more likely than alone.

Why not the scene's decider alone (as for Stalchild): in a large scene the decider may be far away with the pot not loaded, and the player who broke it would get nothing.

**Pickups and blinking out.** A drop of ours that is off the list for two walks, with our Link within 2.5 m of where it last was, was picked up; with Link within 15 m (`LICHII_COOP_DROPS_NEAR`, 0 for never) it blinked out; further away it was only unloaded here and nothing is told. Three or more gone in one walk or within 0.5 s, or no Link on the list, is an unload. A pickup or a blink-out goes out as `DROP_TAKE` 0.5 s later (a scene change or an unload in that time calls it off) and every game deletes its own actor of that drop. Drops are not announced before the clock offset to the server is known. The game gives the collector the item itself; rupees, hearts and ammo stay per player.

**A player who comes in later** sends `DROP_ASK` with its scene at its first walk there. Every game in that scene answers 0.5 s later with `DROP_SPAWN` (flag "again") for each drop it still shows, where it lies now; a game that knows the id ignores it, the newcomer makes each at once.

At most 20 drop messages a second leave one game (the rest wait their turn), so a field of cut grass cannot use up the server's limit.

**Not proven: nothing here has run in a game** (no game was allowed to run while this was written; the unit tests pass and the emulator builds with it). To check with two games:

1. A killed enemy and a broken pot leave the same item at the same place in both games, and the log shows `coop: drops: sent ...` in one and `got ...` in the other.
2. Whether a drop made by the create request can be picked up, gives the item, and blinks out like a real one.
3. Picking one up in either game removes it in the other within a moment (`sent take` / `got take`, `deletes asked`).
4. A drop left alone blinks out in both; a player walking away does not take it from the other (if it does, drops unload nearer than 15 m: lower `LICHII_COOP_DROPS_NEAR`).
5. A player entering the scene later sees the drops lying there.
6. That drops have no placement hash (else the log says `is placed ... not shared`).
7. Leaving a scene through a door right beside a drop: the drop stays for the player who stays (a take is held 0.5 s and called off by the scene change or by more drops going at once).
8. A short loss of the connection in a scene with drops: after it, each drop is there once (an actor already lying is kept as the drop the others tell again), and pickups are still shared.
9. The cost of the drops' own walk of the actor list every second frame (beside the objects walk): frame time with and without `LICHII_COOP_DROPS=0`.
10. Bush and grass loot in Kokiri Forest (#102): cut a bush with a heart or rupee under it; the other player sees it too (each game has its own), one picks it up and it is gone in the other game (`placed ... picked up here` / `placed ... taken in another game`, `taken elsewhere, deleted here`).

Known limits: two players picking the same drop up within the network delay both get it. Two different things that drop within 3 m and 3 s of each other in two games count as one. A drop unloaded here is not made again when we come back near it.

## Protocol proposal

All on the channels [protocol.md](protocol.md) reserves: channel 2 (reliable) for ownership, spawns, removes and damage, channel 4 (unreliable, sequenced) for object samples. Type numbers are given when the code is written, after whatever other stages have taken by then (the next free is 17 today).

An **object id** is 10 bytes: `u8` kind (0 placed, 1 made), `u8` maker player (0 for placed), `u64` value (the placement hash, or the maker's counter). Every message that names an object starts with it, after the player field where there is one.

| Type | Sent by | Body |
|---|---|---|
| `OBJECT_PRESENT` | client | `u8` player field, scene (string), `u16` count, then per object: object id, `u8` flags (bit 0 awake, bit 1 in a fight, bit 2 targeting our player). Sent on every change and every 2 s; it replaces the sender's previous list for that scene |
| `OBJECT_OWNER` | server | object id, `u8` owner (0 none), `u16` epoch, `u32` group (0 none: the group root's index on the server) |
| `HANDOVER_ASK` | server | object id, `u16` epoch, `u8` to player |
| `HANDOVER_STATE` | client, then server | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch, then sample components (below). The old owner sends it; the server relays it to the new owner and then sends `OBJECT_OWNER` to all |
| `OBJECT_SPAWN` | both | `u8` player field, object id (kind 1, maker = sender), actor kind (string, the game's actor name, up to 64), `u8` flags (bit 0 projectile), then sample components; `OBJECT_OWNER` is implied: the maker, epoch 0 |
| `OBJECT_SAMPLE` | both | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch, then the components below |
| `OBJECT_REMOVE` | both | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch, `u8` reason (0 gone, 1 dead, 2 exploded, 3 hit, 4 picked up), position (3 x f32) |
| `OBJECT_GROUP` | client | `u8` player field, `u16` count, then per entry: member id, root id, `u8` flags (bit 0: the member is a boss). Not relayed |
| `DAMAGE` | both | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch the hitter knew, `s32` amount (0 or more), `u8` cause (0 unknown, 1 sword, 2 arrow, 3 fire arrow, 4 ice arrow, 5 light arrow, 6 seed, 7 bomb, 8 nut, 9 boomerang, 10 hookshot, 11 hammer, 12 magic, others kept), position (3 x f32) |

Object sample components, the same scheme and lengths as `PLAYER_SAMPLE`'s where they share an id:

| Id | Name | Data | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `time` | `u64` server time, `u32` sequence | 12 |
| 2 | `position` | 3 x f32 | 12 |
| 3 | `velocity` | 3 x f32, metres a second | 12 |
| 4 | `rotation` | 4 x f32 quaternion | 16 |
| 7 | `animation` | as in `PLAYER_SAMPLE` | 2 + string bytes + 8 |
| 16 | `life` | `s32` life, `s32` max life | 8 |
| 17 | `fuse` | f32 seconds left (bombs and bombchus) | 4 |
| 18 | `state` | `u8` flags: bit 0 in a fight, bit 1 held by a player, bit 2 asleep | 1 |

`time` is required. The owner sends `OBJECT_SAMPLE` at 20 to 30 a second while an object moves (60 for a boss), and once a second while it is still, so silence always means "late". Receivers place them like player samples: 100 ms behind, up to 150 ms of extrapolation.

What the server does:

- Keeps, per object: which games have it present, owner, epoch, group, the fight timer, the last relayed sample.
- Runs the rules above on every `OBJECT_PRESENT`, `PLAYER_STATUS`, `PLAYER_LEFT` and once a second, from the player samples it already relays (for distances) and the owners' samples.
- Relays `OBJECT_SAMPLE`, `OBJECT_SPAWN` and `OBJECT_REMOVE` only from the owner of that epoch (or the maker, for a spawn), to the players that have the object's scene; anything else is dropped.
- Relays `DAMAGE` only to the current owner; drops it when there is none. A `DAMAGE` with an old epoch still goes to the current owner: the hit was real.
- Forgets an object when no game has it present and its owner removed it or left.
- Sends a player who enters a scene (at its first `OBJECT_PRESENT` there) every `OBJECT_SPAWN` still alive in it, at the last relayed state: the made enemies, and placed ones an owner announced. Before this a Stalchild made half a second before a player came in never appeared for that player (issue 101 logs).
- A made enemy (not a projectile) that another game lists in `OBJECT_PRESENT` is handed over like a placed one, and goes on with that game when its owner leaves the scene; with nobody else it is removed as before.
- Says the owner of everything a player lists again every 10 s, since a game that reloads the same scene forgets its table without the server noticing. Does not take a scene back from a late message: `PLAYER_SAMPLE` and `OBJECT_PRESENT` come from different threads and can cross at a scene change, which dropped and re-owned every object twice. A stream that changes its scene is believed at once (so a quick walk out and back loses nothing); one that only repeats the scene it said before, while the other stream has moved on, is ignored.

The server's per-player limits (60 a second on channels 0 to 2, 240 on 3 and 4) are enough for a room of enemies at 30 a second plus projectiles; a busy fight is measured before they change.

## Cutscene NPCs (issue 200, branch `npc-anim`)

A player test: while one player was in a cutscene, the others saw the cutscene's NPCs walk their normal routine. Townspeople (`Npc_`) were already shared, but with whichever game the server picked; the game in the cutscene kept its own NPCs for itself (the 8 m rule below), so the others copied the owner's routine instead.

The rule (`Sync::SetEvent`, `Sync::EventNpc`): the NPCs are synced, not the cutscene. Players outside a cutscene keep playing.
- While our Link is in an event (a cutscene or a talk, `event_running`), every shared townsperson within 30 m of Link (`Sync::kEventNpcM`) is flagged targeting in `OBJECT_PRESENT`. The server gives a copy flagged so to us after 0.5 s (`handover_chased_us`); one we own already is held (a fight lock). From then on every other game's copy plays our NPC's motion: place, rotation, AS command and frame, smoothly, as any shared object.
- Until the hand-over lands, the owner's routine is not put on our claimed copies (no place, play or remove), also beyond the 8 m event rule.
- 2 s after the event (`kEventNpcKeepUs`) the flag drops; the fight lock (12 s) then the normal distance rule decide the owner again. The copies stay where our NPC was left, or follow its routine back.
- NPCs no player is in an event with stay as before. No new message; types 390 to 394, claimed for this branch, are not used.
- Logs: `coop: npc-anim: our Link is in an event: N NPCs near it claimed`, `M of the N claimed NPCs are ours now` (each time the hand-over count changes: the claim is only a wish, the server may hand them over 0.5 s later or keep one in another game's fight lock) and `the event's NPCs are free again` on the game in the event; on the others `coop: npc-anim: <name> copied from P<n> at x y z plays '<anim>' frame f` when an NPC copy's owner changes, and once a second for each NPC copy with `LICHII_COOP_DEBUG_NPC=1`. Autotest: `tools/autotest/scenarios/npc-anim.A.txt`: A talks to Dampe in the Graveyard (he walks his rounds; in the talk he stops and turns to A) after B, standing by him, owns him by the distance rule; B's owner of him must go P2 to P1 right after A's `talk` mark.
- Side effect, not fixed: any event (a signpost, a chest, a shop talk) claims every townsperson within 30 m, not just the ones it moves. If B starts its own talk within 30 m of A's cutscene NPCs, B also drops the owner's wishes for them while its event runs (A keeps them by the fight lock), so B sees them on B's own routine until its talk ends.
- Not known: whether a story cutscene makes its own NPC actors (no placement hash) instead of moving the placed ones. Those would not be shared at all, and would need the spawn sync.

## Waiting for our event (issue 144)

A player test in the Deku Tree: B beat a Deku Scrub and was in its talk. A beat the same scrub in A's game, and B was stuck until A had finished A's own talk. A's kill reached B's scrub during B's talk, and B's event waited on an actor that had changed.

The rule, for every shared change made in this game to an actor (`lichii/hook/coop_guard.h`):
- **What waits.** While our own Link is in an event (`PlayerReading::event_running`: a talk, a cutscene), nothing within 8 m of Link (`CoopGuard::kEventNearM`) is changed by another game. An actor whose place is unknown waits too. Which actor the event is with is not read, so the distance stands in for it.
- **Kills and hurts** (life writes) are kept, one per actor, and made right after the event ends, in the order they came:
  - Two held hurts add up (both hits land). A hurt behind a written life becomes its target, written as life (`CoopGuard::MergeLife`).
  - Each is kept with who was at the address (placement hash and name). It is made only if this walk still has that actor there. A long event deletes and makes actors and the address may hold another one by then: that write is dropped, so it can never hit a pot or an NPC.
  - When it lands, the Sync is told (`Sync::Written`): the drop is that write, not a hit of our Link's. Otherwise, after more than 1 s, it went back to the owner as DAMAGE and the enemy lost the life twice.
  - A scene change drops them, because those actors are gone (enemies come back with the scene).
- **Removes, places and played animations** are only left out while the event runs. The sync makes them again every frame, so they happen right after it.
- The chest rule (a chest within 3 m of our Link is never removed, `CoopGuard::kChestNearM`) uses the same distance test.
- The log has `coop: objects: our Link is in an event: changes to N actors near it wait until it ends`, then `the event ended: N waiting changes made now, M dropped (another actor there now)`, or `the scene changed: N waiting changes dropped`.
- **Long events freeze what is near.** `event_running` is also true while our Link plays the Ocarina, and through a boss intro or any long cutscene. For all that time every shared actor within 8 m keeps its own place and animation (no poses, plays or removes from the others) and takes no kill or hurt. It catches up when the event ends. Not a block. The flag is read each frame, and no reading counts as no event, so it cannot stick on by itself.

Not covered here: the dungeon flags held while in a dungeon (dungeon-live). That is the same idea for flags, applied by the flag store, not by actor.
