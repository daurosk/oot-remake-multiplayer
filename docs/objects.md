# Shared enemies and objects: research and protocol proposal

Plan stage 5 ([plan.md](plan.md)): bombs, arrows and other projectiles seen on every screen, one owner per enemy with a real hand-over, and hits on a copy sent to the owner as damage events. This page says which actors the game uses, where their state is, how an actor is told apart on two games, and proposes the messages and the ownership rules. No product code yet.

Addresses are offsets into `main` of build `e0f3ccfaf50065ae` (1.1.1). **Checked** means read from the instructions or the data files. **Inferred** means taken from a name, a shape or the engine family. **Live** means seen in the running game; see "Live check", the only part that was run.

## Results

- Every player projectile is its own actor, created through the same function as everything else (0x372a70, [puppet.md](puppet.md)). Shot ones go through the shootable create at **0xc16cf0**, which passes the blackboard values `Shootable_KeyHash` and `Shootable_OverrideParamNum` with the request.
- The actors: `NormalArrow` (arrows, also the actor fire, ice and light arrows are shot as: inferred), `Shootable_DekuSeed` (slingshot), `Shootable_Hook` and `Shootable_HookLong` (hookshot and longshot tip), `Item_Bomb`, `Item_Bombchu`, `Item_DekuNut`, `Weapon_Boomerang`. The `Obj_*` and `Item_Arrow_*` packs of the same names are pick-ups.
- An actor's name is a `char*` at actor **+0x260**. Its life is an s32 at **`*(Life + 0x1c10) + 8`**, its maximum at `*(Life + 0x1c18) + 8`, where Life is component-table entry **+0x2b8** (table at actor +0x270).
- A placed actor's 64-bit placement hash, the key of its flag in [flags.md](flags.md), is a u64 at **`*(actor + 0x3d8) + 0x88`**. A game-made actor has `0xffffffffffffffff` there (live).
- Live (2026-11-08): the actor list is found (`[[main + 0x4e91888] + 0x118] + 0x670`, node at actor + 0x538); `Shootable_DekuSeed` is confirmed; the life chain reads right on every enemy; damage is applied by the Life component's step 0x49d8c0, and writing life directly holds and runs the game's own death.
- Proposal: projectiles stay with the game that made them for their whole life and are drawn elsewhere as harmless copies; enemies have one owner with a fight lock and a hand-over that carries the state; any life lost on a copy goes to the owner as `DAMAGE`. Eight new messages (below).

It was found by searching the heap for the player actor's address: one hit is the player's own +0x548, a node whose owner field points back at the player. The list changes while it is read from another thread: a walk sees actors drop out and come back within 0.1 s. The hook's reader drops any walk whose length is not the head's count and calls an actor gone only after two walks without it. Two walks were not enough (churn-b, 2026-11-15): in the 0.11.2 big run (adult and child Kakariko) and in players' 0.11.0 logs, live townspeople, cuccos, fairies, pots and grass left the walk and came back every 0.3 to 1 s, even 11 m from Link, and each time the server handed the object to the other game (hundreds of `OBJECT_OWNER` a minute, two games swapping one shop NPC about 230 times in 90 s). On Hookshot the game's own delete requests are known (patch 0007), so a placed object that leaves alive, owned and with no delete request is *away*, not gone (`Sync::SetDeleteEvents`): it stays listed in `OBJECT_PRESENT` with its last flags and keeps its owner for 2 s (`kAwayKeepUs`); back on the walk it goes on as before, a delete request ends it at once, and after 2 s off the walk it is gone (log `gone <name>: off our walk 2 s with no delete request: left`). An away object is not in the object table, so nothing is written to it. The emulator keeps the two-walk rule.

Watching the list stands in for hooking 0x372a70: an actor made by a create shows up in the list a frame or two later, so names and positions are the same; only the create's arguments (the shooter, the override list) are not seen. That needs the SVC hook.

### Damage (how life changes)

- **The function.** The Life component's step **0x49d8c0** applies damage. Each frame it sums what was dealt to the actor into **Life + 0x1cb0** (from **Life + 0x228**, **Life + 0x1350** and one more term), then sets life with a compare-and-swap at **0x49dcf8**: life minus that sum, at most max, never below 0. A GDB write watchpoint on an enemy's life stops there every frame, with sum 0 when nothing hit.
- **Life + 0x228** is a per-frame damage accumulator: 0xaa920 (called from 0x2c23e28) swaps it to 0 each frame. What adds a hit into it was not found: there is no atomic add to that offset in the code, so it is written through a base other than the Life component. A watchpoint cannot follow it live: the field is written several hundred times a second and each stop starves the frame loop (52,000 stops, no frame shown, in 90 s).
- **Life + 0x1ca8 and + 0x1cac** are a mirror of life and max, rewritten 1 to 2.5 s after life changes (0x8d96a0). They read -1 for an instant now and then. They do not guard life.
- **A real hit.** A player killed a Poe with the Kokiri Sword while a logger read it: life 3 to 2 to 1 to 0, one per hit, and the sum at Life + 0x1cb0 read 1 after each. 2 s after life reached 0 the Poe left the list and `Obj_Spirit_Poe` (its soul) appeared; its lantern `Weapon_PoeCantera` went with it. That Poe was made by the game near a dug-up grave (hash `0xffffffffffffffff`), so it is a game-made enemy: the "Made by the scene with no hash" row of the id table applies to it.
- **A direct write holds and kills.** Through the GDB stub: a ReDead's life 12 to 5 stayed 5, and its mirror followed. 5 to 0: 2.4 s later an `Obj_Rupee_Green` appeared at the ReDead's place, the game's own death drop. The ReDead stayed in the list in state 4, life 0, for the 20 s it was watched. So the owner can apply `DAMAGE` by writing life (the proposal's plan), and the game's own death follows; a copy's life must not be written to 0 by anyone but its owner.

## Telling one actor from another on two games

| Kind | Id on the wire | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Placed (enemies, pots, chests, switches) | the u64 placement hash | Both games load the same Banc placement, and the hash is the key the flag store already uses ([flags.md](flags.md)) |
| Made by a player's game (projectiles, an enemy a player's action spawned) | the maker's player number and a u32 counter of its own | Nothing else names it on both sides; the maker says "I made 7" and everyone calls it (2, 7) |
| Made by an enemy (bullets, summons) | the same, made by the enemy's owner | The enemy's owner is the only game whose copy really fires |
| Made by the scene with no hash (spawners, waves; the night Stalchild now go by "Rolled spawns" below) | kind placed, value = `SceneSpawnId(scene, actor name, order)`: FNV-1a 64 of the three with the top bit set. `order` counts the enemies of that name seen in this scene visit, first seen first (one walk ordered by x, then z) | Stalchild were seen with hash ~0 (Alex's test, 2026-11-09). The n-th Stalchild in game A and the n-th in game B get the same id, so a hit on one shows on the other, even when each game spawned its own near its own Link. Positions are not shared yet, so the two may stand in different places; a later rule can make the scene host's game the only spawner and hide the others' extras |

A placed id is only good inside one scene, since a scene can be loaded on one game and not the other. Messages that name a placed object say its scene once, in `OBJECT_PRESENT`.

**The rule** (branch `objects-6`, `coop_objects_sync.cpp`, tested in `objects_test.cpp`):

- Each game knows the others' scenes from their `PLAYER_SAMPLE`s (a scene older than 5 s does not count). The **decider** of a scene is the lowest player number in it. Only the decider's rolls are kept.
- **Far apart, each rolls its own** (issue 124, branch `boss-targets`; `Sync::RollerFor`, tested in `objects_test.cpp`). The game spawns Stalchild around its own Link, so with one decider for the whole field a player far from player 1 got none. Now a roll is kept by the lowest player number whose Link (its last `PLAYER_SAMPLE` position, under 5 s old) is within 40 m of where the roll rose (`kRollNearM`); a player in the scene whose place is unknown or old counts as near (the rule before). With no lower-numbered player that near, a game keeps and shares its own roll as a made id with itself as maker, and the others make copies of it as of the decider's. The `rolls decided by player N` line still names the lowest number in the scene.
- On the decider, a new hashless `Enemy_Stalchild` or `Enemy_Stalchild_Big` gets a made id (maker = the decider, its own counter) and is sent once as `OBJECT_SPAWN` (name, place, rotation, animation, life); then samples as for any enemy it owns, and `OBJECT_REMOVE` (dead or gone) when it leaves the list.
- On every other game in the scene, its own roll is deleted on the next walk with the game's delete request (0xa6490), the same way as a taken pickup, and counted. The spawner is not stopped, so it may roll again and be deleted again: the log line `rolls decided by player N, C copies to make, R own rolls deleted` shows how often.
- The decider's `OBJECT_SPAWN` makes the spawn director (`CoopSpawn::Director::SetEnemies`, keys with the top bit set, `place` off) create the same actor by name at the decider's spot. When it is on the list it takes the decided id; from then on the Mover places it where the decider's one is and plays the decider's animation at its frame (so the rise from the ground is the decider's rise). A fresh Stalchild within 4 m of a copy still being made is not deleted for 3 s, since it is likely that copy before the director lists it. At the decider's `OBJECT_REMOVE` the director deletes the copy.
- The director needs the svc cave (`LICHII_COOP_PUPPET` or `LICHII_COOP_SPAWN`), as the Mover does.
- **A copy that sinks by itself is made again** (issue 124). In the chunk 2 test (`stalchild` scenario, 2026-11-09) B made A's three Stalchild at A's spots within 0.1 s, but each copy played its own `Disappear` 2 s later and left B's list about 4 s after it was made, while A's fought on for 10 more seconds. So B showed no Stalchild during the fight, and A's kills had nothing to reach in B. B's clock was already night (set to 20:00 4 s before) and B stood about as far from them as A's Link, so the trigger is something else in the Stalchild's battle AI (`Enemy_Stalchild_LandBattle.actionseq` reads a create-time range, a return distance and the user's perception; the `Disappear` module ends in `OneShotGenericDeleteThisActor`). Not traced. A later roll copied at 135 s stayed and attacked. Now: when a decided copy leaves our walk while the decider's one is still there (no `OBJECT_REMOVE` yet), the director makes it again 1 s later, at most 3 times (`CoopSpawn::Copy::remakes`, `Sync::kRemakes`), and the new one gets the 3 s create wait again so it is not taken for an own roll. A copy that left dead (life 0 or a death animation, from a hit here) is not made again. Log: `coop: spawn: Enemy_Stalchild: copy <key> went by itself; made again (n of 3)`, or `not made again` after the third. A copy waiting to be made again is not made if by then the input says remakes 0 (it died here: the director sees it go at once, Sync knows it died two walks later).
- **This is a stop-gap.** The copy's own AI still runs: the remade copy has the same reason to sink and may do it again about 2 s later, so 3 remakes cover roughly 10 s of a fight. The real fix is one of: (a) hold the copies' AI (`LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_AI_HOLD`, "Holding a copy's AI", still off and unproven, and it holds every copy, not only Stalchild), or (b) find what in `Enemy_Stalchild_LandBattle.actionseq` sends a Stalchild to `Disappear` (its create-time range or the user-perception check) and keep that false for copies. Many `not made again` lines in a game test mean it is time for (a) or (b).
- Not done: a decider who leaves the scene (its made ones are then gone for the server too, and the next player starts rolling); the copy's rise starts when `OBJECT_SPAWN` arrives, so it is about one round trip later than the decider's (the Mover then sets it to the decider's frame).

### Hatched enemies (Gohma's larvae, issue 178)

Issue 178, a player test in the Deku Tree: Gohma's eggs killed as eggs were shared, but once one hatched it stayed an egg on one screen and attacked as a larva on the other.

**Why.** An egg and its larva are two actors (`Enemy_Gohma_Egg` or `Enemy_DungeonBoss_Gohma_Egg`, then `Enemy_Gohma_Larva`; each has its own `AS` file). Neither has a placement hash in the Deku Tree (the scene places one `Enemy_Gohma_Egg`, no larva; Gohma drops the rest), so both go by `SceneSpawnId`. Only the egg's owner runs it; every other game's copy is held in the owner's animation and cannot hatch. So the egg went (alive) and a larva came in the owner's game only: no game had a rule to end the egg copy or make the larva elsewhere.

**Now** (`IsHatchingEgg`, `IsHatchling`, `Sync::Hatch`; tested in `objects_test.cpp`, gohma-larva cases):
- A fresh `Enemy_Gohma_Larva` within 3 m of an egg this game owns (still on the walk, or gone alive at most 2 s before) came out of it. It gets a made id (maker = this game) and is sent once as `OBJECT_SPAWN`, as a rolled enemy is; then samples and, when it leaves, `OBJECT_REMOVE` dead or gone. Log: `share hatched here: Enemy_Gohma_Larva`.
- The egg is ended with `OBJECT_REMOVE` reason gone (with its epoch) once both have happened. An egg of ours that left alive stays on our `OBJECT_PRESENT` list for those 2 s, since the server hands an unlisted object to another game at once and would then refuse the remove. This also holds back the hand-over of an egg that left our walk alive without hatching (we walked away, the room unloaded) by those 2 s. An egg that left with life 0 or in a death animation is a kill, not a hatch (the kill rules above).
- Other games: `OBJECT_REMOVE` gone for a placed egg (epoch not older than they know) deletes their copy at once (`share hatched in its owner's game: <egg>`), and the made `OBJECT_SPAWN` of a larva makes the spawn director create one where the owner's is (as for rolled spawns). A fresh larva within 4 m of a copy still being made waits for it, so it is not taken for a larva of their own.
- A larva next to an egg another game owns (its copy hatched by itself) keeps the old placed id: not shared as made. A larva with no egg of ours near is the same.
- Since boss-larva-0116 (issue 178 still seen in the 0.11.4/0.11.5 Gohma runs): the eggs Gohma lays in the fight (`Enemy_DungeonBoss_Gohma_Egg`, no placement hash) were matched between games only by order (the Nth egg in the scene), but each game's copy Gohma laid its own: the copy game had 1 or 2 eggs to the owner's 3, and an egg laid while the other game owned Gohma stood on that screen only and never went. They are now Gohma's minions (`MinionMaker`): only her owner's game keeps them, shares each as a made `OBJECT_SPAWN`, and the others make that one (a copy's own create is stopped, or deleted). ## Design

### Projectiles

- **The maker owns it, for its whole life.** No hand-over: an arrow lives under a second, a bomb a few. This is the TotK lesson in reverse: there, arrows and bombs were never synced and only their damage was, which showed nothing on the other screen. Here both are done.
- **Copies are harmless.** On other screens a projectile is drawn with a small pack of our own (`CoopShot_Arrow`, `CoopShot_Seed`, `CoopShot_Bomb`, `CoopShot_Bombchu`, `CoopShot_Nut`, `CoopShot_Boomerang`, `CoopShot_Hook`), built like the puppet's: the game's own model, animation and effect files by path, no physics, sensor, chemical, attack or AI. A copy can never hit, explode, light a torch or pull Link. So nothing it does needs to be undone, and no hit is counted twice.
- **What the maker sends.** `OBJECT_SPAWN` once, reliable, with the actor kind, position, velocity, rotation and its server time; then `OBJECT_SAMPLE` while it flies, for the ones that do not fly straight (bomb rolling or held, bombchu, boomerang, hook tip), at up to 30 a second; then `OBJECT_REMOVE` with the reason and the place (hit, exploded, gone). An arrow and a seed need no samples: the copy flies them from the spawn's position and velocity, with gravity (to measure: the arrow's physics param), until the remove.
- **Explosions and hits shown.** On `OBJECT_REMOVE` with reason exploded, the copy plays the bomb's explosion effect and sound at that place and disappears; with reason hit, an arrow copy sticks there for a moment and a seed or arrow gets its hit spark and sound there (impact actors, [spawns.md](spawns.md#where-a-shot-lands-issue-166)). The effect's name comes from `Item_Bomb`'s `ELink` and `SLink` files (not read).
- **Held bombs (issue 169).** See [spawns.md](spawns.md), "Held bombs and the fuse".
- **What projectiles do to the world.** A bombed wall, a shot eye switch, a lit torch: what is saved goes through shared flags ([protocol.md](protocol.md), stage 4). What is not saved (a timed switch, a torch) is not shared at first and is listed under "Not done".

#### What is built (branch `projectiles`, issue 85)

The copies are the game's own actors made by the spawn sync ([spawns.md](spawns.md), `LICHII_COOP_SPAWN=1`), except seeds, arrows, hookshot tips and boomerangs. Since issue 85 those are the `CoopShot_Seed`, `CoopShot_Arrow`, `CoopShot_Hook`, `CoopShot_HookLong` and `CoopShot_Boomerang` stand-ins ([spawns.md](spawns.md), "Seeds, arrows and hookshot tips"); the other `CoopShot_*` packs above are not made. On top of it, `lichii/hook/coop_projectiles.{h,cpp}` (tested in `tests/lichii/projectiles_test.cpp`) holds the rules below, used by `coop_spawn.cpp` and `coop_objects_sync.cpp`. No new message: everything rides on `OBJECT_SPAWN`, `OBJECT_SAMPLE` and `OBJECT_REMOVE` components that protocol 4 already has (rotation, fuse), so the protocol version stays.

| Kind | Actor | Copy's path | Its end |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bomb | `Item_Bomb` | samples (held, thrown, rolling) | blown up at the owner's fuse; `exploded` if it lived 1 s |
| Bombchu | `Item_Bombchu` | samples | blown up at the owner's remove |
| Deku nut | `Item_DekuNut` | samples, carried on along its arc | deleted; `hit` if it went at 3 m/s or more |
| Slingshot seed | `Shootable_DekuSeed` | the same; a bounce is sent at once | the same; a `hit` copy stays 0.2 s where it struck ([spawns.md](spawns.md), issue 166) |
| Arrow (all elements) | `NormalArrow` | the same | the same; a `hit` copy sticks 1.5 s where it struck |
| Boomerang | `Weapon_Boomerang`, shown as `CoopShot_Boomerang` (the game's own one would home on this game's enemies) | samples (it homes and turns back) | deleted |
| Hookshot, longshot tip | `Shootable_Hook`, `Shootable_HookLong` | samples, with the tip's animation (its model is the chain, `Item_Hookshot_Chain`; the chain's length is its animation set's `ChainExtend`) | deleted |

- **Turned the owner's way.** The maker reads the 3x3 at actor +0x328 with the place and sends it as a quaternion in the spawn and every sample. The copy is created with that turn in the create argument, and each game frame the director writes place and turn as one 48-byte block at +0x31c, then calls the actor's own vtable slot 0x218 `(actor, 0)` (0x4c6580 on every actor seen; the director checks the slot points into main), as `ExecuteActorForceSetMtx` does, so the physics takes the new place. `LICHII_COOP_SHOTS_SETTER=0` leaves the call out.
- **The same path at the same time.** A copy is placed where the owner's projectile is *now*: the latest sample carried on by its velocity for the time since the sample's server time (at most 250 ms), with the fall that two samples show (velocity y lost per second, 0 to 60 m/s²) for arrows, seeds and nuts. Before, it was carried on by the time since the message arrived, so a copy ran one network delay behind.
- **Smooth flight** (branch `smooth-shots`). In a hand test other players' shots looked choppy: the copy was carried on from the latest sample by the velocity the owner measured between two walks, and that swings (0 or twice the speed when the owner draws 60 frames a second and its game moves the shot 30 times), so the copy stood still or jumped and snapped at each sample. Now a copy is shown 100 ms late on the server clock, as late as the puppet that shot it, between the two samples around that time (`Track::Shown`), so it moves the same each frame and leaves the puppet's hands, not ahead of them. Only when no sample is that recent is it carried on as before (with the fall, at most 250 ms). A hit goes on to where the owner's shot struck (taken a walk after its last sample) and stays there; every copy the owner removed (hit or gone) is kept 100 ms longer, so it is seen reaching the hit spot or the hand before its land hold starts (a nut flashes at the wall, a boomerang comes back to the hand); a bomb that goes off does so at once where the owner's did. A thrown bomb no longer jumps 100 ms ahead when it leaves the puppet's hands.
- **Explosions at the same moment.** The maker learns its game's bomb fuse from its own bombs (the middle of the last 7 that lived 1 to 10 s; log `coop: shots: our bombs' fuse is ...`). From then on its bombs' spawn and samples carry `fuse` (seconds left). Another game blows its copy up when the server clock reaches the sample's time plus that fuse, without waiting for the remove (`coop: shots: bomb copy blown on the owner's fuse`); the remove still blows it up if it comes first (a bomb hit by fire), and the first bomb of a session, with no fuse known yet, goes off at the remove as before.
- **No hit taken twice.** A copy bomb is a real bomb in its game, so it hurts that game's enemies too. Each copy that goes off (by the owner's fuse, its remove, or by itself) is noted with its place and time. The objects sync then treats a life drop within 6 m of it, from 0.2 s before to 1.2 s after, as that blast's: on a copy it is not sent as `DAMAGE` (the owner's own bomb deals it there), and on an enemy we own it is kept as credit for 2 s, which a `DAMAGE` from the thrower's game for the same bomb uses up first (log `coop: shots: life drop by a projectile copy's blast kept back`). When the `DAMAGE` comes first (no fuse known yet, so the copy goes off at the remove, one network delay late: each player's first bomb of a session, and every bombchu), it is kept as debit for 2 s and the copy's blast drop after it is written back up by that much. A copy bomb removed as `gone` (under 1 s) is no blast.
- **Why it went.** `OBJECT_REMOVE` says `exploded` for a bomb or bombchu that lived 1 s, `hit` for a nut, seed or arrow that left the list at 3 m/s or more (it struck something), else `gone`.
- **Arrow elements.** Fire, ice and light arrows are all `NormalArrow` (inferred). The shooter's element is already known to every game from its `PLAYER_EQUIP` arrow; `ElementOf` and `CauseOf` turn it into a damage cause. The copy is a plain arrow: how the game gives an arrow its element was not found.

Not proven: nothing of this has run in a game. The credit and debit are per enemy, not per thrower: on the owner, its own bomb beside another player's copy blast within 2 s of a `DAMAGE` can cancel against it (one hit lost or put back). Copy arrows and seeds that hit an enemy copy are not guarded (blasts only). Thrown from the puppet: the copy starts where the owner's projectile was made (the owner's hand), and the puppet stands at the owner's place 100 ms late, so the two should meet; not seen.

#### Enemy shots (branch `enemy-shots`)

Each game runs the AI of its copy of a shared enemy, and a copy plays the owner's animations, so every game's Octorok spat its own rock (aimed at its own Link) and none was the other's. Now the game that owns the enemy fires, for everyone. `lichii/hook/coop_enemy_shots.{h,cpp}` (tested in `tests/lichii/enemy_shots_test.cpp`) holds the rules; the spawn sync carries the shots like a player's projectiles (no new message, no protocol change). `LICHII_COOP_ENEMY_SHOTS=0` turns it off (each game its own shots, as before).

- **Which names.** From the game's `Pack/Actor`: `Octorok_Bullet`, `Dodongo_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossDodongo_Bullet_*`, `ScrubDeku_DekuNut`, `ScrubForestStage_DekuNut`, `SkullKid_Bullet`, `PoeHyrule_Bullet_*`, `FlareDancer_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossGanondrof_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossPhantomGanon_Bullet_*`, `PhantomGanon_Bullet_*`, `Twinrova_Bullet_*` and `KotakeKoumeTwinrova_Bullet_*` (not the `_MirrorShieldReflect` ones: Link's), `Ganon_Bullet_*`, `Ganondorf_Bullet_*`, `DungeonBossGanon_Bullet_*`, `Enemy_MoblinClub_ShockWave` (taken out of the objects sync's enemies). Each has its maker's name (`Enemy_Octorok`, `Enemy_Scrub_`, `Enemy_DungeonBoss_Dodongo`, ...). Beams, breath and auras (`BeamosBeam`, `Enemy_DungeonBoss_Barinade_Beam`, `DungeonBossVolvagia_FireBreath`, `Bubble_Green_Bullet_Aura`) are not shots and stay each game's own. Only `Octorok_Bullet` and the scrub nut are in the brief's test; the others are names only.
- **Whose shot.** After each of its walks the objects sync notes every shared enemy with its place and owner (ours, another player's, not known). When the spawn sync first sees a new enemy shot, the nearest enemy of its maker's name within 10 m (30 m for a boss or mini-boss) decides: ours, the shot is sent (`OBJECT_SPAWN` with the projectile flag, samples at up to 30 a second, `OBJECT_REMOVE`); another player's, it is a copy enemy's shot and is deleted; no maker found or no owner known yet, it is left alone and not sent. Log: `coop: shots: enemy shot Octorok_Bullet at x y z: sent (our enemy's)` / `deleted (a copy's; the owner's comes)` / `kept here (no owner known)`.
- **The copy hits.** The other games make the owner's shot as the game's own actor (no stand-in), placed on the owner's path every frame with the setter, so it can hit their Link where it meets him: the player it was aimed at takes the hit in their own game, once. In the owner's game the real shot flies through that player's puppet (a puppet has no body to hit).
- **The delete.** A copy enemy's own shot is given to the director after it was seen on 2 walks (so a copy the director has just made, not yet reported, is never taken for one; a shot is also never claimed as a copy by its place). On the calc thread, just before the game's delete request, it is checked again: still on the actor list (its node's owner is itself), the same vtable as when seen, none of the director's copies, and no create of ours waiting to be taken; each address once. `LICHII_COOP_ENEMY_SHOTS_HIDE=0` keeps them (shared, but doubled), to compare. Log: `coop: enemy shot 0x... of a copy enemy deleted (the owner's is shown)`.

Not proven: nothing of this has run in a game. Whether a shot made by our create request without its enemy flies and hurts (it might wait for its maker, or delete itself; then the copy is gone and the hit is not taken here), whether deleting a copy enemy's shot leaves that enemy waiting on it, and whether the copy's own AI fires on the same frame as the owner's (the delete then comes 2 walks later: the shot can be seen for about 50 ms).

#### Shots sent back (row 177, branch `shot-reflect`, `coop_shot_reflect.h`)

A shot goes back by its own Shootable: Link's sword (Phantom Ganon's and Ganondorf's light ball) or shield (an Octorok's rock, a Deku Scrub's nut) hits it and its state goes to Reflected. A copy never does that (it is placed on the owner's path), and on the owner's game the other player is a puppet with no sword or shield of the game's own, so only the owner's Link ever sent a shot back (row 177: a guest could not return the light ball).

- **Which shots.** The game's own ShootableParam of every shared shot (romfs, build e0f3ccfaf50065ae): `IsMeleeWeaponAttackReflect` for `DungeonBossGanondrof_Bullet_LightBall` and `DungeonBossPhantomGanon_Bullet_LightBall` (and `IsBottleWeaponAttackReflect`); `IsGuardReflect` for `Octorok_Bullet`, `ScrubDeku_DekuNut`, `ScrubForestStage_DekuNut`. No other shared shot says either (fire, shock waves, thunder, Twinrova's balls and beams, Poe, Flare Dancer, Skull Kid). Twinrova's beams go back by the Mirror Shield's own `_MirrorShieldReflect` actor, Link's own. Not covered: `DungeonBossGanondrof_Bullet_ChargeLightBall`, whose own AI asks its sensor (`QuerySensorHitGanondorfChargeLightBallIsReflect`) rather than its Shootable.
- **Ganondorf's charge ball (row 211, not covered).** Read from its two AIs and main (build e0f3ccfaf50065ae). `QuerySensorHitGanondorfChargeLightBallIsReflect` (calc main+0x2adaca0) reads the ball's own taken hits only: `IsReflect` = a hit of kind 2, 3, 17 or 18; `IsReflectReturnToCreator` = that hit's +0x1c4 bit 4. Both are that node's outputs, not blackboard slots. The root AI's top `SelectorBoolTrigger` (node 10) takes `IsReflect` straight from its own copy of that query (node 6); its Triggered branch (node 23) is the reflect: AI-local `IsReflected` = true, `IsReflectedToCreator` = the query's second output, the reflect target, effect and layer change. The NotTriggered branch switches on `QueryShootableGetState`: 1 idle, 2 flying, anything else sets the velocity to zero, so `RequestChangeStateReflected` would only stop this ball. The post-sensor AI writes the component blackboard bools `DungeonBossGanondorf_Bullet_ChargeLightBall_IsCandidateMostRichReflect` (bool 0, from `IsReflectReturnToCreator`) and `..._IsCandidateMostRichHit` (bool 2, from `QuerySensorHitGetAtHitInfo` `IsHit`). Ganondorf's `ExecuteDungeonBossGanondorfResolveChargeBulletEffectFlag` (calc main+0x2699e60) walks his linked charge balls and sets `..._IsMostRichReflect` / `..._IsMostRichHit` (bools 1, 3) on the first candidate. It uses the blackboard of main+0xd6d110(actor), the getter main+0xd6cee0(bb, key) and the setter main+0xd6cd10(bb, key, value, 0). Only the ELink reads the MostRich bools, so one ball of the burst shows the effect. Setting any of these bools does not send the ball back. Only a hit in the real ball's own sensor does. No other actor uses that query or those keys (all actor packs searched).
- **The copy's game sees it.** The director reads its copy's own Sensor component (actor +0x270, component +0x3b8): for a melee shot its taken hits (+0x68, 0x1d0 bytes each), a hit whose kind (+0x90) is one the game's own reflect query takes (kind 2, 3, 17 or 18: mask 0x6000c) from our Link (the attacker handle +0x60, or +0x78 when +0x95 bit 0 is set); for a guard shot its dealt hits (+0x70, 0xa0 bytes each), one our Link guarded (+0x0b bit 0, the defender handle +0x78). A handle's actor is [[handle+8]+0x40]. Once per copy it sends `DAMAGE` with cause 14 (reflected), amount 0, to the shot's owner.
- **The owner's game sends it back.** The spawn sync takes `DAMAGE` cause reflected for a shot of ours; the shot's record is looked up in the Shooter component (+0x3d8) of each of our shared enemies (its map at +0x20, +0x2c slots of 0x38 bytes keyed by a u32; +0x8 count, +0x10 records of 0xf8 bytes, the shot's handle at +0x20), and the game's own `Shooter::RequestChangeStateReflected` (main+0x2eb3bd0, shooter, key, index) is called on the calc thread (caller order 11). That function asks `ChangeState(record, 4)` (main+0x3036470), the shooter's update applies it, and the shot flies back as for the owner's own Link; its copies follow its path as always.
- **Solo and idle.** Nothing is read or asked without a copy of another player's shot or a `DAMAGE` from another player.

Not proven in game yet: that our Link's swing is on the copy's taken hits (the copy is Stationary and placed every frame), and where `RequestChangeStateReflected` sends a shot with no guard direction (the game's own reflect target).

### Enemies

The rules answer each way the TotK sync broke ([protocol.md](protocol.md) "Rules it is built on"; the TotK port's `docs/online-sync.md`, section 3).

- **Who may own.** Only a game that has the enemy loaded and awake. Each game says what it has in `OBJECT_PRESENT`.
- **First owner.** The first game to report the enemy present. Two at once: whichever list reaches the server first; if the other player is the one near it, the distance rule below moves it 3 s later.
- **Fight lock.** An enemy is "in a fight" from the first change of its life, or from its owner's report that it is targeting a player, until 12 s after the last of those (60 s for a boss). In a fight it keeps its owner. It moves only when the owner leaves the session or the scene, unloads it, is loading, has been paused over 6 s, or has been dead over 10 s; or by the hit rule below. A boss whose owner's samples hold one animation, action verb and life for `boss_stall_us` (40 s) is stuck in that move (row 149): its fight lock is freed and not renewed by its samples until the move changes (`boss_stalled`).
- **Out of a fight.** It moves to another player only if that player is within 30 m of it and at least 10 m nearer than the owner, and either the owner is over 40 m away or that player is within 20 m, for 3 s in a row, and not within 5 s of the last change. The 20 m part was added for issue 101: an enemy's AI chases only its own game's Link, so the game of the chased player is the only one that moves it right. In that run the owner stood 34 m from two Peahat larvae that chased the other player, and "owner over 40 m" alone would have left them with it.
- **A chase takes it** (issue 101, branch `enemy-chase`). The distances above are to the owner's enemy, which stays home while another game's copy chases that game's Link far away; so no distance ever saw the chased player near it. Each game now tells the server which copies chase its Link (`OBJECT_PRESENT` bit 2, targeting), and out of a fight, not within 5 s of the last change, the object goes to the game that has said so for 500 ms (the longest chase first, then the lower player number), wherever the owner's enemy stands. A chase on a group's part moves the root (and the part with it). The owner flagging its own enemy as targeting is a fight, so an enemy that chases its owner's Link stays: when both games' enemies chase their own Link nothing moves.
- **A hit can take it.** A `DAMAGE` from a player who is within 30 m of the enemy (of the owner's enemy, or of where the hit landed: the hitter's copy, which may have chased the hitter far from the owner's enemy, issue 101) and at least 10 m nearer than its owner moves it to that player at once (not within 5 s of the last change), fight or not: the enemy fights whoever hits it. Two players in the same fight are never 10 m apart in their distance to it, so their hits do not flip it, which is what the TotK sync got wrong. The hit itself still goes to the old owner first, and the life the new owner gets is never above the life before the hit minus the hit, so a hit that crossed the hand-over is not lost.
- **No quick hand-back** (player report 37: in Revirie's 0.11.0 log an Octorok went P2, P3, P2, P3 ... 42 times, several within 10 s, and the players saw enemies flicker between them). A hit, a chase (issue 101) or the near-player rule does not give an object back to the player who gave it up until `handover_back_us` (15 s) after that change; a frozen owner, a group's root, a fish and a boss keep their own rules. Counted as `handover_back_held` (each check that held it).
- **Sticky target** (player report 138: in Revirie's 4-player 0.11.4 log an enemy's owner changed 2,791 times, 566 of them within about 5 s of the last; Keese went 2, 3, 2, 1, 3, 4, 2 ... and every change turns the enemy to another player's Link, because its AI chases only its owner's): the hand-back rule above holds for *every* player who gave the object up in the last `handover_back_us`, not only the last one, so 3 or 4 players cannot pass it round. And an object that changed owner within `handover_back_us` stays with its owner while that owner stands within `handover_chase_m` of it (the owner is its target): a chase, the near-player rule or a hit from another player waits until the owner walks off or the time is over. With two players nothing changes (the only other player is the one who gave it up). When an owner lets go of an object (its game unloaded it, it left the scene), a player who can play takes it before a loading, paused, dead or downed one, who would only hand it on again a moment later (the log: a Lizalfos went 1, 3, 2 within a fraction of a second).
- **Groups.** A boss and its parts, and an enemy and what it fired or summoned, are one group with one owner. A group's root is the boss (or the summoner); parts follow it. Part lists come from the actor names (`DungeonBossDodongo_Bullet_*` follows `DungeonBossDodongo`, and so on), checked for each boss when its fight is tried. The game that knows the names tells the server with `OBJECT_GROUP` (member, root, a boss flag); the server holds no names. A member is never moved alone: it follows its root's owner, and a sign of a fight on any member is the root's fight. A projectile stays with its maker until it is gone, whatever its group.
- **Hand-over carries the state.** The server asks the owner to let go; the owner answers with its last state (position, velocity, rotation, life, animation command and frame); the server passes it to the new owner and only then announces the change. If the owner is gone or has not answered within 500 ms, the server uses the last sample it relayed. If the player it was for has left or unloaded it meanwhile, nothing changes. The new owner places the enemy at that state advanced by the velocity over its age, and sets its life to the lower of its own and the received life, so a boss never heals across a hand-over.
- **An owner epoch.** Each change of owner adds 1 to the object's epoch. Every sample and damage event carries it, and a receiver drops anything from an older epoch. A late sample from the old owner can then never pull the enemy back. One thing from the epoch before is still taken, for 2 s: the old owner's `OBJECT_REMOVE` with reason dead, since its enemy really died before it heard of the change; it is passed on with the new epoch, the new owner included.
- **Copies.** A non-owner's enemy is the game's own actor (it is placed, so it exists anyway). Each frame the hook writes the owner's interpolated pose into it, as for the puppet, and its life from the latest sample (never above it). Its AI still runs; holding it still is a later step (TotK's `TOTK_SYNC_AI_HOLD` idea), measured first.
- **Only the owner removes.** A copy whose life reaches 0 here is held at 1 until the owner's sample says 0, so the enemy dies once, on every screen, when its owner says so. The owner's `OBJECT_REMOVE` (reason dead) removes copies. A non-owner unloading its copy only sends `OBJECT_PRESENT` without it. An owner's enemy that leaves its walk with no delete request (the game streams enemies out as Link moves on: the 133 Keese lines in Revirie's 0.11.4 log of report 138 were that, each leaving with the Amos, Baby Dodongos and Gold Skulltulas of the same part of the cave) is away, not gone (churn-b). One the game itself deleted with no death read (life 0 or a death animation) is gone for good: `OBJECT_REMOVE` gone after 1 s, and the copies are deleted (enemy-left-0116; [protocol.md](protocol.md)); not while the game deletes 12 or more actors within 1 s (the scene going), not for a kind whose delete input reads our own Link (`lichii/hook/coop_area_deleted.inc`, generated by `tools/puppet_pack/area_deleted.py` from the romfs: of 129 enemy delete inputs only Water Temple's `Enemy_ShellBlade` and `Enemy_Tektite_Blue` read our Link's sensor, the rest read flags, the time of day, a challenge step or a timer, the same in every game), and not when our Link is over 20 m from it (an AI's own despawn for our Link). A delete request for an address that is away tells nothing (the actor is not run off the list, and the address may be another's by then). While an event plays here the away time does not run out (2 s again from its end, at most 30 s off the walk): DC's second Lizalfos pair left the list for over 2 s in its jump-in event (FocusOnEnemy_02), was let go and handed to player 2, and our game then made copies of "player 2's encounter" beside its own (row 138(3), Revirie's log).

### Damage

One path for every kind of hit, as TotK's `TOTK_SYNC_DAMAGE` found worked best (`docs/online-sync-ws3.md` there): read the life of every enemy copy right after the hook writes it, and again at the next frame hook, after the game's own frame. A drop is a hit taken on this screen.

- Drops on one copy are summed for 100 ms from the first, then sent once as `DAMAGE` to the owner, with the amount, the cause if known and where it hit.
- Not sent: when the owner's own life for it came down by the same amount since 50 ms before (both games ran the same hit), when our player is over 80 m away, or over 20 a second.
- The owner takes the amount off its enemy (life at least 0) and sends a sample at once. Its life change starts the fight lock, but does not move the enemy (above).
- Hits that change no life (a Deku nut's stun, a boomerang's stun, a hookshot pull) are not seen by a life read. They need the cause sent instead: `DAMAGE` with amount 0 and the cause, applied by whatever the game does for that cause. Not found yet.
- Projectiles hit only on their maker's screen, so a remote player's arrow reaches a copy on the maker's game, and that game forwards the drop. Copies of projectiles never hit.
- Puppets have no life (players hurt each other only with PvP on). Enemies do target puppets (issue 150, on by default since perception-on: the pack gives each puppet a perception emitter, [puppet.md](puppet.md)): the owner's enemy may pick another player's puppet by the game's own rules. Its attack on a puppet itself does nothing (no body); the chase rule below hands the enemy to that player, whose game then fights its own Link. A puppet whose player cannot be fought now (downed, spectating, paused, in a menu, loading, in their own event, dead) is not perceived at all ([dungeons-boss.md](dungeons-boss.md), "Every player is a target").

## The hook side (first cut)

`LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS=1` turns it on (off by default). `lichii/hook/coop_objects_sync.cpp` holds the rules, tested in `tests/lichii/objects_test.cpp`; `coop_objects.cpp` walks the actor list every second frame and writes lives. Only actors whose name starts with `Enemy_` are shared.

- `OBJECT_PRESENT` lists the enemies loaded in our scene (the scene of `PLAYER_SAMPLE`), on every change and every 2 s. A new scene forgets every id.
- The owner sends `OBJECT_SAMPLE` (time, position, life) at most 20 times a second while life or place changes, once a second while still.
- On a game that does not own an enemy, a drop of its life is our hit: the drops over 100 ms go to the owner as one `DAMAGE` (cause unknown). The owner writes life minus the amount (not below 0) into its enemy, and its next sample carries it.
- A copy takes the owner's life from samples: a lower life at once, a higher one only when none of our hits is in flight (500 ms after our `DAMAGE`), so it does not heal back between our hit and the owner's answer. `HANDOVER_STATE` gives a new owner the last life.
- Animals are shared the same way (`Animal_` names, so a cucco's hits and place are shared), except the ridable Epona (`Animal_Epona_Adult`, `CoopRide::kHorse`): each game keeps its own, and the ride sync shows the other player's horse under its puppet (epona-ride; shared, one game's Epona was a frozen copy under its rider, or the other game drew two horses).
- **Copies follow the owner** (branch `objects-4`). The owner's samples also carry rotation (the 3x3 matrix at +0x328 as a quaternion), velocity (from the change of place between walks) and the animation (AS slot 0: command, frame, rate; a new command alone is worth a sample). Every other game works out, each presented frame, the owner's pose 100 ms in the past, blended between the two samples around that time or carried on by the velocity for at most 150 ms past the last. A copy with no sample for 2.5 s is left to itself; an enemy we own is never moved. `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_POSE=0` turns this off.
- **Moving an actor needs the game's own setter.** A plain write of +0x31c from the emulator is undone within a frame (two-game test, 2026-11-09: copies were 4.8 m off on average, up to 71 m). The AI node `ExecuteActorForceSetMtx` (run 0x2622e50) writes +0x31c..+0x34b and then calls the actor's vtable slot 0x218 with `(actor, 0)`. Every actor seen has the vtable 0x4b25e18, whose slot 0x218 is **0x4c6580**: it sets bit 23 of actor +0x4a8 and hands the new transform to the physics component (table +0x50, 0x4c6630 and 0x4c6870). So the hook's Mover, a caller of the svc cave (`CoopSvc::AddCaller`, order 30, after the puppets, spawn copies and fairies), does the same on the game's calc thread each game frame for every copy, after checking that the actor is still on the list (its node points back at it), awake (state 4) and has that vtable. Live: copies held exactly where they were put (0.000 m off over 1,488 moves in 5 s). The cave is patched only when `LICHII_COOP_PUPPET` or `LICHII_COOP_SPAWN` is on.
- **Animations and hit reactions.** On copies the Mover plays the owner's AS slot 0 command through the game's play request (0x2022a0, as for the puppet) and checks the copy every game frame on the calc thread: when its slot 0 shows another name (the copy's own AI took over), the owner's one is played again at once and started at the owner's frame (0x37b5d70 `(controller, 0, s0 = frame)`, as the puppet), the sample's frame plus rate x 30 a second, shown 100 ms late like the pose; on each new sample a copy more than 3 frames off is set to it. Before (Alex's hand test, 2026-11-09) the replay came every 250 ms from frame 0, so a cucco near the other player and a carried cucco looked frozen in a loop (`Lifted` replayed 42 times in 11 s). Not yet seen live with this change. The names live in one block from the game's malloc (0x14c6c90), one fixed cell per name, since a slot may keep the pointer. Live: a ReDead hit by A's sword showed the same place and pose in B, and B's copy played `Standing`, `Turn`, `Run`, `Wait`, `Attack_Start`, `Attack_Fail` right after A's. The red hit flash is not an animation and is not copied.
- **Life on copies is written directly.** The Life step 0x49d8c0 reads an s32 at Life + 0x228 (and Life + 0x1350), stores their sum at Life + 0x1cb0 and takes it off life; but Life + 0x228 belongs to a whole hit record (+0x1f0..+0x2f0: a present byte at +0x24c, a hit id at +0x248 compared with the last at +0x1b18, the attacker at +0x240..+0x26c, copied to +0x1b20.. as the last hit). An amount written there alone was never taken (live). `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_HURT=1` still tries it, with a direct write after 400 ms; off by default. 0xaa920, which clears Life + 0x228, is the Life reset, not a per-frame step. Hit records seen live (A's ReDead, Kokiri Sword): +0x1f8 `00060001 40040000`, +0x200 the hit id (0xbc, 0xbe, 0xc0), +0x218 the hit actor itself.
- **One-time pickups taken elsewhere.** A key64 flag arriving set to 1 (`FLAG_SET`, `FLAG_STATE` or the snapshot) marks that placement key taken; a loaded actor with that placement hash (a Gold Skulltula, its `Obj_RupeeToken`) is no longer shared and is deleted once with the game's delete request (0xa6490 `(actor, 0)`) by the Mover. Live with a test key (the ReDead's hash): B deleted its ReDead 0.4 s after, A kept its own. Not yet run with a real Skulltula.
- Test switches (two-game tests only): `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_TEST_DRIVE=1` holds one of player 1's owned enemies 3 m ahead of Link, swaying, hurt by 1 every 4 s; `=2` holds it still 2 m ahead for real sword hits; `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_TEST_TAKE=<hex key>` acts on player 2's game as if that key arrived taken, 3 s after sharing starts.

The items they sell stay shared.
- **Fish and lures** (issue 151, fishing-2, fishing-3): fish (`Animal_Fish_*`, `CoopObjects::IsFish`) are shared animals, and the owner game is the one whose lure is nearest the fish. `Sync::SetLures` gets our own cast lures each walk (the spawn sync's `OwnLures`, through `CoopProjectiles::TheLures`). Each walk ends with `FISH_LURE` ([protocol.md](protocol.md), "Fish and lures"): every fish we have loaded, how far our nearest lure is from it (25 cm steps, none past 6 m), *hooked* when we own it and it is within 1 m of our lure, *drawn* when we own it and it comes at our lure at 0.2 m/s, or when it is a copy whose own AI swims it 5 mm a frame or more towards our lure (or holds within 1 m: a bite) for 1 s (no gap over 400 ms). The server gives each fish to the game whose lure beats the owner's (1 m nearer, or drawn when the owner's is not and not over 1 m farther; held 300 ms, not within 1 s of the last change) and keeps a hooked fish with its owner, even while that game is paused or a list is late (10 s). Only the owner's hooked counts. A copy's AI may turn at a far lure while the owner's fish circles a near one, so drawn alone never takes a fish from a nearer lure (the review of fishing-3: otherwise the fish went back and forth every 1.3 s). So the fish is ours before it reaches our lure, and our game runs the approach, the bite and the fight. A fish we own that leaves the walk within 3 m of our Link was landed: `OBJECT_REMOVE` dead, and the other games delete their copy at once (no life to lower). Other players' lures are model-only stand-ins ([spawns.md](spawns.md)), so no fish here goes for them.
  - *Why fishing-2 was not enough* (read from the code; fishing-2 never ran past the pond's door in a test). It moved fish with the enemies' rules: a copy that swam at our lure was flagged targeting (the chase rule), and the owner's fish at its own lure was flagged too, which the server takes as a fight. (1) That fight lock lasts 12 s after the last flag, so a fish that had been near A's lure could not go to B for 12 s, while B's copy bit B's lure and was pulled back each frame. (2) Out of a fight the distance rule gave a fish to whichever player stood 10 m nearer to it, wherever the lures were, so a fish swimming at A's lure could go to B. (3) The hand-over waited for the copy's own AI to swim at our lure for a second, which a fish that circles before it bites may never do. Now a fish is never flagged targeting or fighting, the server moves fish only by the lures, and the nearest lure wins before the fish starts its approach.
  - Left: a copy whose AI bites our lure while the owner's lure is nearer still bites a fish the Mover pulls back (both lures within a metre or two of one fish). A copy landed in a game that does not own it is not removed elsewhere (the server takes a remove from the owner only). The thresholds (6 m, 1 m, 0.2 m/s, 5 mm a frame) are not measured.
- **Townspeople** (branch `objects-5`): `Npc_` actors are shared like enemies and animals (place, rotation, animation, life), except each player's own: `Npc_Navi` (the fairy sync has her), `Npc_Equipment_*` (held equipment) and `Npc_Invisible_*` helpers, which the first Kakariko run showed being shared. - **Guards** (branch `guard-sync`, issue 191; tested in `objects_test.cpp`, not yet run in a game). Guards were shared as townspeople, but the hand-over rules (chase, wake, the Mover's put-back) were for `Enemy_` actors only. A guard's AI sees only its own game's Link, so a copy near the other player spotted that player with its own AI while the Mover kept playing the owner's patrol over it: the guard stood still or walked on in one game, and the spotted player's reaction was seen nowhere else. Guards are `CoopObjects::IsGuard`: the stealth guards that throw a child out (`Npc_Hylia_Soldier_SecurityGuard` in the castle garden, `Npc_Gerudo_GEN_SecurityGuard`, `Npc_Gerudo_FortressGuard`; their AI has `Notice`, `NoticeTurn_Quick`, `Discover`, `Surprise` and an alert gauge) and the town soldiers (`Npc_Hylia_Soldier_*`, `Npc_Hylia_ImperialSoldier_*`, who `Salute` and greet). A guard copy within 25 m (`kGuardWakeM`) of our Link whose own AI plays a reaction (`IsGuardReaction`: Notice, Discover, Surprise, Alert, Found, Salute, Greeting, Talk), seen by the walk or put back once by the Mover, for 1 s, is flagged targeting, and the server gives the guard to us; a guard of ours reacting near our Link is flagged too, so it stays ours. Its patrol (walk, wait, turn), which differs between games only by timing, never moves it. The guard that becomes ours gets the release and its own AI's reaction played again (issue 157), so it finishes spotting our Link in our game: only the spotted player is thrown out (that player's game runs its own event), and the other player sees the guard's notice and discover through the samples. The guard comes back to the other game by the usual rules once the spotted player is taken out of the scene.
- **Hit replay** (on by default since Alex's Stalchild test; `LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_HIT_REPLAY=0` writes life directly instead): a life an enemy must lose, on a copy (the owner's sample has less) or on the owner's own enemy (a `DAMAGE` came), is written into its Life component on the calc thread as a whole hit, shaped like a live Kokiri Sword hit (attack header +0x1f8, amount +0x224/+0x228, incoming hit +0x240..+0x2d8 with our player's actor as the attacker, a fresh hit id, the enemy's place; the list links from +0x2d8 on untouched). The game's own damage step then takes the life and plays the reaction: the flinch, the knockback, a Stalchild's head coming off (`Damage_Head`). The attacker's place in the record is where the player who hit stands (from their `PLAYER_SAMPLE`, not older than 2 s; the owner's place for a life that came down in a sample), so the knockback goes away from them. Only `Enemy_` actors: an animal, a pot or a crate gets its life written. One that could not be given within 200 ms is dropped. **An enemy's life is never written** (enemy-replay-native, the owner's native rule; bosses since boss-intro): a hit the game has not taken after 400 ms goes to the game's own Life step (Hookshot, patch 0007: the step at main+0x49d8c0 adds it to that frame's damage, then clamps, reacts and runs its own death), but only once the enemy's damage step has seen our replayed hit (its last hit id, Life + 0x1b18, reached ours) and refused it; while the replay still waits (the actor asleep or paused) nothing more is given, so its step takes the replay itself and one hit is never dealt twice. With no Life step (the emulator), a full table, or a given change no step takes in 250 ms, the hit is lost: the enemy keeps its game's life (log: `not taken by its game's own steps` / `its Life step did not take the change: lost`, with a running count). If the game still takes a hit after its wait, the fall is no hit of ours (not sent on); an animal, a pot or a crate is put back, an enemy keeps it. An enemy copy is never written back up to its owner's life either (a revive, a hit the owner refused): it follows the owner only down. A killing hit of ours given after the wait says our enemy dead (and deleted 3 s later if it stands on, issue 133) only once the walk reads its life at 0 within 1 s; a lost kill leaves it alive in every game. A copy dead here whose owner still shows life after the two resends stays split (noted `dead here, owner still shows life after the resends`, counted by `SplitKills`). Why: in Alex's run (2026-11-09, `alex-logs/run2`) a hit in the game that did not own a Stalchild reached the owner as a bare life write (hit record all zero, no `Damage_Head`), and 0.5 s later the owner's `NoWeaponAttack` was played over the hitter's own flinch. So also: for 700 ms after our own hit on a copy, the owner's animation from before the hit is not played over it; a new one (the owner's reaction) is. Earlier live result with the switch on: B's game took 4 of 5 replayed hits through its own damage step. Not yet seen live as the default: the reaction on the owner's screen, and the head.
- **The real hit (protocol 14, every hit shows on every screen).** The stand-in sword record above is now only the fallback (an older peer, a record not read, places not finite). The hitter's walk reads the hit its own game took on its copy (Life +0x240, 38 words, `ReadHitRecord`: attacker pointer and present cleared) whenever the copy's last hit id moved to one we did not replay, and sends the newest one of the summed hit with the `DAMAGE` (`hit_record`). The owner's replay takes ONLY its hit kind (words +0x250 up to +0x27c, plain values; review-pr-d crash guard: nothing else of another process's record, which holds pointers and links) into the stand-in record and the attack header, with our player as the attacker, a fresh id and the places as before (`hit_at` when exact), so the owner's enemy takes the same hit: a bomb on King Dodongo, an arrow or a seed on Gohma's eye, fire, ice, the hammer. Every hit the owner's game took (its own, or the replay) goes in its samples for 500 ms as component 21 (`hit`, a seq per object): a third game's copy replays that record with the life fall of the same sample, once per seq. The hitter's own copy already took its own hit, so the owner's sample has no fall for it there and nothing is given twice. Bosses and brain-path copies: the replay runs only the damage step (flash, sparks, sound, life); a replayed id is never our own hit, so no own brain window starts (`NewHits` returns before `NewOwnHitAt`); the flinch's verbs stay the owner's. Log: `copy ... replays a hit of N (id ..., the hit the other game took)` or `(..., the stand-in sword hit)`. Open: a hit that took no life on a plain enemy (a nut's or boomerang's stun) still sends no `DAMAGE`; a no-life hit on a boss copy still runs its own stun window (issue 150).
- **Whose fall it is: the hit id, not the life (branch g2-bosses).** While a replay waits, a fall in that actor's life was booked as the replay landing; a boss copy's refused replay is asked again by every owner sample, so it waited all fight and our own hits on it were never sent. Now the walk reads its Life component's last hit id (+0x1b18, ids only grow): moved to an id we did not replay is our game's own hit (`Seen::own_hit`, every actor with a Life component), and that fall is ours (sent as `DAMAGE`); a replay reached in the same walk takes its part of the fall first. Log `our own hit while a replay waited`, 5 s line `own hits kept while a replay waited N`. The same rule the other way for PvP (`GivePvp`, `CoopPvp::LinkLifeWatch`): a PvP hurt waiting on our Link was taken whenever its life reached the expected one, so a monster's hit in that wait used it up and a refused replay lost the PvP hit; now a fall whose hit id is not our replay (a monster's) lowers every waiting expected life by that fall, and a rise (a heart, a fairy, a revive) raises it, so the written fallback never takes a heal back. Tests: `tests/lichii/objects_test.cpp` ("while a replay waits", "TestOwnHit"), `tests/lichii/pvp_test.cpp`. Not seen in a game: the hit id moving for a monster's hit on our Link (read from the same Life component as an enemy's). A fall with no hit id (a fire floor, a fall) during a PvP wait is held one read (its id may be read a walk late) and then booked as harm, or as the landing with the harm of that read if our replayed id came; a read that holds takes no new PvP asks, so a held fall is booked only against the hurts waiting when it was held. Hurts waiting together expect lives one after the other (`CoopPvp::NextExpect`), so one landing or written fallback never books another. Left as is: a replay refused (its id, no fall) followed next read by a fall with no id books that fall as the landing (the written fallback is then not needed: never dealt twice, at worst the smaller of the two is lost); a heal read in the same walk as the landing raises the expected life (rare: the written fallback then deals the PvP hit again).
- **Every copy gets its call** (branch `enemy-copies`). The cave made at most 16 game calls a frame for all callers, and the Mover asked one per copy first: in Alex's run (2026-11-09, `alex-logs/run3`, 19 to 21 copies in Hyrule Field) exactly 16 of them were moved each frame, always the same ones, the rest never (a Peahat larva up to 32 m off), and no call was ever left for an animation ("0 plays"). - **A change of owner** (same branch). An `OBJECT_OWNER` with an older epoch than the one known is dropped, and so is a sample from an older epoch. When the owner changes, the samples kept from the old one are dropped, so the copy goes to the new owner's place with its first sample. An enemy that becomes ours stays where it stands here, takes the lower of its life and the handed-over one (never a higher), sends its sample at once, forgets a hit of ours not yet sent (it is already in its life) and ignores our own `DAMAGE` if the server hands it back to us.
- **Holding a copy's AI** (same branch; read from the code, nothing run). Two ways the game itself stops an actor's AI were found:
  - *Pause.* The AI node `OneShotRequestPauseOwn` (run 0x291e980) calls **0xa6740** `(actor, type)`, beside the delete request 0xa6490: it sets bit `type + 10` of the u32 at actor +0x30 (six pause kinds, bits 10 to 15) and the actor goes to state 5. A paused actor does not run at all, so its animation would stand still too; the Mover also only moves actors in state 4. Not used.
  - *Brain skip.* The BSA component (the enemy's "brain": which verb it is in, such as idle, search, battle) is component-table entry **+0xb0** (0xcad060). `QueryBsaIsBrainSkip` (run 0x29ce250) reads bit 0 of its u16 at **+0xc4**. A loop over all BSA components (around 0x369b7c to 0x36a070) rewrites that word every frame: bits 0 to 3 come from a virtual call on the object at manager +0x950 (slot 0x40 or 0x48, at 0x369df8), the rest from the manager's own state (+0x930, +0x9f0) and the component's u64 at +0xb8. So a bit written from outside is likely replaced before the brain reads it.
  - Who reads the bit: 0xcc010 and 0xd89a4 load the actor's BSA component and test bit 0 of +0xc4 (`tbz`, the clear case taking the same path as an actor with no BSA component). Which way it works (set = this frame's brain is skipped, as the query's name says, or set = it runs) could not be told from the code alone.
  - **The hold** (`LICHII_COOP_OBJECTS_AI_HOLD`, off by default; `coop_hold.h`, `coop_hold.s`, patched in by `coop_svc.cpp` beside the co-op cave). The call at **main+0x369df8** (`blr x8`, the one whose answer gives bits 0 to 3) becomes `bl 0x3c46300`, a 76-byte cave that makes the same call and then, when the component in `x21` is in a table at main+0x3c46400, changes the answer to `(bits & and) | or`. The table (count, and, or, up to 64 components) is written by the Mover at the start of every game frame from `Sync::Holds`: the `Enemy_` copies we do not own that the owner's samples place right now. An enemy that becomes ours, or whose owner fell silent for 2.5 s, is not in the next table, so its own AI runs again from that frame. Mode `=1` sets bit 0 on held copies, `=2` clears it.
  - What a run shows: the loader line `Applying "coop ai hold"`, and every 5 s `ai hold mode M: N copy-frames held, brain skip bit as asked in K; P plays in S s`. K equal to N proves the cave reaches the copies' components. The hold works in the mode where P (animations the Mover had to put back because the copy's AI chose its own) falls to near 0 and the copies stop turning to and attacking this game's Link; compare with a run without the variable. If neither mode does it, the brain is gated elsewhere: next to read are the two readers above and the pause request.

- **Which copies chase our Link** (branch `enemy-chase`, issue 101; `Sync::NoteDrift`, tested in `objects_test.cpp`). Each frame, before the Mover puts the copies back, it reads where the game's own frame left each one. An `Enemy_` copy we do not own that its own AI pulls at least 2 cm a frame towards our Link, within 40 m, with no gap over 400 ms, for 1 s, is chasing us: `OBJECT_PRESENT` flags it targeting for 2 s after the last such step, and the server gives it to us (above). - **Kills and woken enemies** (branch `enemy-kills`, issues 133 and 132; tested in `objects_test.cpp`, nothing run in a game). A player test in the Deku Tree by Lixyas (2026-11-10, protocol 4): a Skullwalltula killed with the slingshot stayed alive in the other game, a big Skulltula's attack was not always seen there, and in the first room only one Deku Baba rose in one game. The Deku Tree autotest logs show both games list the same Deku Babas and Skulltulas under the same placement hashes, and that the big Skulltula (`Enemy_Skulltula`) has no life we can read (no `life` line in either game), so neither a hit on it nor its death went through life. What changed:
  - *An owner does not outlive the enemy* (the main cause, found in the players' own logs). The server forgets an object that no game has loaded and makes it anew at epoch 0. The hook kept the owner it last heard for a placed id after the enemy left its walk, and dropped every later `OBJECT_OWNER` with a lower epoch as late. In the Lixyas logs the Skullwalltula (`0x282f9be0f907855f`) went to player 2 at epoch 1 and back to the server's fresh epoch 0 with player 1; player 1's hook kept "player 2, epoch 1" for 7 minutes. So player 1 never sent its enemy's state, and its slingshot kill went out as `DAMAGE` to the owner, which the server drops when it comes from the owner itself. **How the other games' own random drop is replaced, and why.** The game's own roll is not stopped: where the game decides a drop was not found, and a patch there could not be tried while no game may run. Instead every roll is let happen and the actor list is watched, the way the rolled Stalchild are handled:

- A drop actor that is new on the list (not there at the scene's first walk, not a copy) is announced once with `DROP_SPAWN`: its own id (maker and counter), name, place and server time. The flag "owned" is set when something this game owns (an enemy, a pot, a crate: `CoopObjects::OwnedActors`) stood within 3 m of it in the last 5 s.
- Two announcements from different games within 3 m and 3 s of each other are the rolls for the same thing. One stays: the one flagged owned, then the one from the lower player number. Every game works this out from the announcements alone, so all end with the same drop whatever order the messages came in (tested for a third game both ways).
- A game whose own roll lost keeps that actor as the winner's when it is the same kind (nothing is seen to change), else deletes it with the game's delete request (0xa6490, through `CoopDrops::Remover`, svc caller 40, which first checks the address still holds a live actor of that name). It waits 0.5 s before making it, so its own roll can be kept instead.
- So a thing drops the same item at the same place everywhere. When the winner's game rolled nothing but another game rolled something, that one stays for everybody: a drop is a little more likely than alone.

Why not the scene's decider alone (as for Stalchild): in a large scene the decider may be far away with the pot not loaded, and the player who broke it would get nothing.

Three or more gone in one walk or within 0.5 s, or no Link on the list, is an unload. A pickup or a blink-out goes out as `DROP_TAKE` 0.5 s later (a scene change or an unload in that time calls it off) and every game deletes its own actor of that drop. Drops are not announced before the clock offset to the server is known. The game gives the collector the item itself; rupees, hearts and ammo stay per player.

**A player who comes in later** sends `DROP_ASK` with its scene at its first walk there. Every game in that scene answers 0.5 s later with `DROP_SPAWN` (flag "again") for each drop it still shows, where it lies now; a game that knows the id ignores it, the newcomer makes each at once.

At most 20 drop messages a second leave one game (the rest wait their turn), so a field of cut grass cannot use up the server's limit.

**Not proven: nothing here has run in a game** (no game was allowed to run while this was written; the unit tests pass and the emulator builds with it). To check with two games:

1. A killed enemy and a broken pot leave the same item at the same place in both games, and the log shows `coop: drops: sent ...` in one and `got ...` in the other.
2. Whether a drop made by the create request can be picked up, gives the item, and blinks out like a real one.
3. Picking one up in either game removes it in the other within a moment (`sent take` / `got take`, `deletes asked`).
4. A drop left alone blinks out in both; a player walking away does not take it from the other (if it does, drops unload nearer than 15 m: lower `LICHII_COOP_DROPS_NEAR`).
5. A player entering the scene later sees the drops lying there.
6. That drops have no placement hash (else the log says `is placed ... not shared`).
7. Leaving a scene through a door right beside a drop: the drop stays for the player who stays (a take is held 0.5 s and called off by the scene change or by more drops going at once).
8. A short loss of the connection in a scene with drops: after it, each drop is there once (an actor already lying is kept as the drop the others tell again), and pickups are still shared.
9. The cost of the drops' own walk of the actor list every second frame (beside the objects walk): frame time with and without `LICHII_COOP_DROPS=0`.
10. Bush and grass loot in Kokiri Forest (#102): cut a bush with a heart or rupee under it; the other player sees it too (each game has its own), one picks it up and it is gone in the other game (`placed ... picked up here` / `placed ... taken in another game`, `taken elsewhere, deleted here`).

Known limits: two players picking the same drop up within the network delay both get it. Two different things that drop within 3 m and 3 s of each other in two games count as one. A drop unloaded here is not made again when we come back near it.

## Protocol proposal

All on the channels [protocol.md](protocol.md) reserves: channel 2 (reliable) for ownership, spawns, removes and damage, channel 4 (unreliable, sequenced) for object samples. Type numbers are given when the code is written, after whatever other stages have taken by then (the next free is 17 today).

An **object id** is 10 bytes: `u8` kind (0 placed, 1 made), `u8` maker player (0 for placed), `u64` value (the placement hash, or the maker's counter). Every message that names an object starts with it, after the player field where there is one.

| Type | Sent by | Body |
|---|---|---|
| `OBJECT_PRESENT` | client | `u8` player field, scene (string), `u16` count, then per object: object id, `u8` flags (bit 0 awake, bit 1 in a fight, bit 2 targeting our player). Sent on every change and every 2 s; it replaces the sender's previous list for that scene |
| `OBJECT_OWNER` | server | object id, `u8` owner (0 none), `u16` epoch, `u32` group (0 none: the group root's index on the server) |
| `HANDOVER_ASK` | server | object id, `u16` epoch, `u8` to player |
| `HANDOVER_STATE` | client, then server | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch, then sample components (below). The old owner sends it; the server relays it to the new owner and then sends `OBJECT_OWNER` to all |
| `OBJECT_SPAWN` | both | `u8` player field, object id (kind 1, maker = sender), actor kind (string, the game's actor name, up to 64), `u8` flags (bit 0 projectile), then sample components; `OBJECT_OWNER` is implied: the maker, epoch 0 |
| `OBJECT_SAMPLE` | both | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch, then the components below |
| `OBJECT_REMOVE` | both | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch, `u8` reason (0 gone, 1 dead, 2 exploded, 3 hit, 4 picked up), position (3 x f32) |
| `OBJECT_GROUP` | client | `u8` player field, `u16` count, then per entry: member id, root id, `u8` flags (bit 0: the member is a boss). Not relayed |
| `DAMAGE` | both | `u8` player field, object id, `u16` epoch the hitter knew, `s32` amount (0 or more), `u8` cause (0 unknown, 1 sword, 2 arrow, 3 fire arrow, 4 ice arrow, 5 light arrow, 6 seed, 7 bomb, 8 nut, 9 boomerang, 10 hookshot, 11 hammer, 12 magic, others kept), position (3 x f32) |

Object sample components, the same scheme and lengths as `PLAYER_SAMPLE`'s where they share an id:

| Id | Name | Data | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `time` | `u64` server time, `u32` sequence | 12 |
| 2 | `position` | 3 x f32 | 12 |
| 3 | `velocity` | 3 x f32, metres a second | 12 |
| 4 | `rotation` | 4 x f32 quaternion | 16 |
| 7 | `animation` | as in `PLAYER_SAMPLE` | 2 + string bytes + 8 |
| 16 | `life` | `s32` life, `s32` max life | 8 |
| 17 | `fuse` | f32 seconds left (bombs and bombchus) | 4 |
| 18 | `state` | `u8` flags: bit 0 in a fight, bit 1 held by a player, bit 2 asleep | 1 |

`time` is required. The owner sends `OBJECT_SAMPLE` at 20 to 30 a second while an object moves (60 for a boss), and once a second while it is still, so silence always means "late". Receivers place them like player samples: as late as the owner's puppet (at least 100 ms, more over a slow line: docs/presence.md, "Each player's own delay"), up to 150 ms of extrapolation.

What the server does:

- Keeps, per object: which games have it present, owner, epoch, group, the fight timer, the last relayed sample.
- Runs the rules above on every `OBJECT_PRESENT`, `PLAYER_STATUS`, `PLAYER_LEFT` and once a second, from the player samples it already relays (for distances) and the owners' samples.
- Relays `OBJECT_SAMPLE`, `OBJECT_SPAWN` and `OBJECT_REMOVE` only from the owner of that epoch (or the maker, for a spawn), to the players that have the object's scene; anything else is dropped.
- Relays `DAMAGE` only to the current owner; drops it when there is none. A `DAMAGE` with an old epoch still goes to the current owner: the hit was real.
- Forgets an object when no game has it present and its owner removed it or left.
- Sends a player who enters a scene (at its first `OBJECT_PRESENT` there) every `OBJECT_SPAWN` still alive in it, at the last relayed state: the made enemies, and placed ones an owner announced. Before this a Stalchild made half a second before a player came in never appeared for that player (issue 101 logs).
- A made enemy (not a projectile) that another game lists in `OBJECT_PRESENT` is handed over like a placed one, and goes on with that game when its owner leaves the scene; with nobody else it is removed as before.
- Says the owner of everything a player lists again every 10 s, since a game that reloads the same scene forgets its table without the server noticing. Does not take a scene back from a late message: `PLAYER_SAMPLE` and `OBJECT_PRESENT` come from different threads and can cross at a scene change, which dropped and re-owned every object twice. A stream that changes its scene is believed at once (so a quick walk out and back loses nothing); one that only repeats the scene it said before, while the other stream has moved on, is ignored.

The server's per-player limits (60 a second on channels 0 to 2, 240 on 3 and 4) are enough for a room of enemies at 30 a second plus projectiles; a busy fight is measured before they change.

## Cutscene NPCs (issue 200, branch `npc-anim`)

A player test: while one player was in a cutscene, the others saw the cutscene's NPCs walk their normal routine. Townspeople (`Npc_`) were already shared, but with whichever game the server picked; the game in the cutscene kept its own NPCs for itself (the 8 m rule below), so the others copied the owner's routine instead.

The rule (`Sync::SetEvent`, `Sync::EventPartner`): the NPCs are synced, not the cutscene. Players outside a cutscene keep playing.
- While our Link is in an event (a cutscene or a talk, `event_running`), every shared townsperson within 30 m of Link (`Sync::kEventNpcM`) is flagged targeting in `OBJECT_PRESENT`. The server gives a copy flagged so to us after 0.5 s (`handover_chased_us`); one we own already is held (a fight lock). From then on every other game's copy plays our NPC's motion: place, rotation, AS command and frame, smoothly, as any shared object.
- Until the hand-over lands, the owner's routine is not put on our claimed copies (no place, play or remove), also beyond the 8 m event rule.
- 2 s after the event (`kEventKeepUs`) the flag drops; the fight lock (12 s) then the normal distance rule decide the owner again. The copies stay where our NPC was left, or follow its routine back.
- NPCs no player is in an event with stay as before. No new message; types 390 to 394, claimed for this branch, are not used.
- Logs: `coop: npc-anim: our Link is in an event: N NPCs near it claimed`, `M of the N claimed NPCs are ours now` (each time the hand-over count changes: the claim is only a wish, the server may hand them over 0.5 s later or keep one in another game's fight lock) and `the event's NPCs are free again` on the game in the event; on the others `coop: npc-anim: <name> copied from P<n> at x y z plays '<anim>' frame f` when an NPC copy's owner changes, and once a second for each NPC copy with `LICHII_COOP_DEBUG_NPC=1`. Autotest: `tools/autotest/scenarios/npc-anim.A.txt`: A talks to Dampe in the Graveyard (he walks his rounds; in the talk he stops and turns to A) after B, standing by him, owns him by the distance rule; B's owner of him must go P2 to P1 right after A's `talk` mark.
- Side effect, not fixed: any event (a signpost, a chest, a shop talk) claims every townsperson within 30 m, not just the ones it moves. If B starts its own talk within 30 m of A's cutscene NPCs, B also drops the owner's wishes for them while its event runs (A keeps them by the fight lock), so B sees them on B's own routine until its talk ends.
- Not known: whether a story cutscene makes its own NPC actors (no placement hash) instead of moving the placed ones. Those would not be shared at all, and would need the spawn sync.

### Every actor the event is with (row 97, branch `event-copy`)

A player's log (Mr. Shorty, 0.11.1, Deku Tree): his Link was held in an event for 15 minutes. Both Deku Scrubs of that room were copies owned by a player who had moved on; the copies played the owner's `Sleep`, put on them every frame by the Mover, because the 8 m rule (`coop_guard.h`) only kept back actors near our Link and the claim above only took townspeople. An event that waits on an actor further away (a scrub's entrance or talk, a shopkeeper, a story event's actor, a mini-game's) then waited for good. The root cause is the same for every kind of event: a copy the event uses kept getting its owner's motion.

The rule now (`Sync::EventPartner`, `Sync::EventPartnerWhy`): while our Link is in an event, a shared actor is our event's partner when
- it is an event member in our Link's event (`coop_event_member.h`, `InSameEvent`): by the game's own event member test (`QueryEventMemberIsEventMemver` 0x29f3770): the actor's EventMember `M = [[actor + 0x270] + 0x88]`, its record `L = [M + 0x18]` (0: in no event), four slots of 0x38 bytes from `L + 0x10`; a member of an event when a slot holds that event's pointer and bit 0 of the slot's byte at `+8`, one of its member slots holds the same event as one of our Link's ([scenes.md](scenes.md) "Whose event"). Any distance, any kind of actor;
- or it has no event member component and its own AI asked for another animation than its owner's since the event began (the Mover's put-back, `NoteTakeover`), within 30 m;
- or it is a townsperson within 30 m (issue 200, as above).

Not bosses: `CoopBossNative::SetEvent` already lets a boss copy run its own changes while our event runs, and the fight keeps its owner. Not fish (the lure rules, issue 151). The own-AI rule is only for actors with an AI of their own (enemies, guards, townspeople), never a world object another game moves (issue 194).

A world object our event binds (a chest it opens, a block or platform it moves) is a member like any other actor: our event drives it, so the owner's place and play are not put on it while the event runs (the glue drops a partner's wishes even where `CoopGuard::Skip` would let an issue-194 world object follow at once). A world object our event does not bind still follows the other game at once, as issue 194 needs.

A partner is claimed exactly as the townspeople are: flagged targeting so the server hands it to us, the owner's place, animation and remove are not put on it, and a kill or hurt from another game waits for the event's end (`CoopGuard::Local::partner`). 2 s after the event (`Sync::kEventKeepUs`) the normal owner rules come back.

Logs (`coop: event:`): `our Link's event began: <event> kind <k> (<n> in the session); names seen: ...` (the kind: 0 flowchart, 1 timeline, 2 movie; the names are the name-like strings the event points at, since the event's name is not mapped), `the actors our event is with: <name> <address> (owner P<n>, bound by the event | its own AI acts | townsperson, <m> m); ...` on each change, `our Link is still in its event after <s> s (<n> actors claimed)` every 30 s, and `our Link's event ended after <s> s`. The `npc-anim` lines now count actors, not only NPCs.

**In our Link's event only (row 97, G1).** In the two scrub runs (20261116-085755, -133559) an `Animal_Butterfly` 26 to 32 m away read as bound during each of our events, and our Deku Scrubs never did. The first fix compared each actor's `[M + 0x18]` with our Link's (`InOurEvent`); that word is each actor's own member record, so it was never equal for two actors and the check never fired. Now the game's own test decides (`InSameEvent`): a butterfly holds the event's pointer without the member flag (`0x6`), so it is never claimed. Once per actor name and event the walk logs `coop: event: <name> <addr>: an event member in our Link's event | not an event member in our Link's event | no event member component`. Not proven in a run: whether a Deku Scrub is a member of our talk with it (the offline run `fable-g1-events` never reached the talk: A died to the brothers' nuts first). If it is not, the 8 m guard (dungeon.md) still holds its flags.

**The event flag itself (row 97 review).** `PlayerReading::event_running` was only the event manager's running word, which stays 0 in a Deku Scrub's talk and its AttackedEvent (scrub-sale-a's runs). It is now that word **or** our Link's own event performer bound into an event (`coop/host/src/player_reader.cpp`, `read_link_bound`: the same performer read as above, on our Link's actor), set in one place so every user sees scrub events: the scrub talk and sale holds, the 8 m guard and the claims here, hiding the others in our event, the `in_cutscene` status we send, PvP (no hits in our talk), teleport (no warp mid-talk), DBNO revives, live gimmick events (wait for our event), the time nudge, quest catch-up and the ocarina mend. Each of those already acts the same in an NPC talk, which sets the word, so a scrub talk now behaves like one. `PlayerReading::event_by_link` says our Link's membership alone fired (the manager's word is 0). Read in the offline run `fable-g1-events`: our Link was a member in every event of its own (scene-load events, a death, the Ocarina take-out) and in none outside them (record 0).

## Walking townspeople (branch `npc-anim-2`, `coop_npc_anim.h`)

The owner's report for 10.1: townspeople walk and run animated on the host's screen but slide in one pose on the others'. The cause, from a Kakariko run's log (2026-11-12): a copy's own AI asks for its own animation every game frame, and the Mover played the owner's back every game frame (over 100 plays in 5 s for one copy), each time from "the owner's frame" read at AS slot +0xe8. That is not the playing frame: the game's getter 0x37b5e70 reads +0x10c. So each play restarted the walk at the same frame.

The fix (module only, no game patch, no new message):
- Townspeople (`Npc_`) send the playing frame (+0x10c) and copies compare with it.
- After the Mover's play on a copy, the slot's name pointer is set back to the name its own AI asked for (the alias). The game's play request skips a name the slot already shows, so the AI's request of every frame does nothing and the owner's walk plays on. It is played again only when the owner changes animation or the AI asks for a different one.

## Waiting for our event (issue 144)

A player test in the Deku Tree: B beat a Deku Scrub and was in its talk. A beat the same scrub in A's game, and B was stuck until A had finished A's own talk. A's kill reached B's scrub during B's talk, and B's event waited on an actor that had changed.

The rule, for every shared change made in this game to an actor (`lichii/hook/coop_guard.h`):
- **What waits.** While our own Link is in an event (`PlayerReading::event_running`: a talk, a cutscene), nothing within 8 m of Link (`CoopGuard::kEventNearM`) is changed by another game. An actor whose place is unknown waits too. Which actor the event is with is not read, so the distance stands in for it.
- **Kills and hurts** (life writes) are kept, one per actor, and made right after the event ends, in the order they came:
  - Two held hurts add up (both hits land). A hurt behind a written life becomes its target, written as life (`CoopGuard::MergeLife`).
  - Each is kept with who was at the address (placement hash and name). It is made only if this walk still has that actor there. A long event deletes and makes actors and the address may hold another one by then: that write is dropped, so it can never hit a pot or an NPC.
  - When it lands, the Sync is told (`Sync::Written`): the drop is that write, not a hit of our Link's. Otherwise, after more than 1 s, it went back to the owner as DAMAGE and the enemy lost the life twice.
  - A scene change drops them, because those actors are gone (enemies come back with the scene).
- **Removes, places and played animations** are only left out while the event runs. The sync makes them again every frame, so they happen right after it.
- The chest rule (a chest within 3 m of our Link is never removed, `CoopGuard::kChestNearM`) uses the same distance test.
- The log has `coop: objects: our Link is in an event: changes to N actors near it wait until it ends`, then `the event ended: N waiting changes made now, M dropped (another actor there now)`, or `the scene changed: N waiting changes dropped`.
- **Long events freeze what is near.** `event_running` is also true while our Link plays the Ocarina, and through a boss intro or any long cutscene. For all that time every shared actor within 8 m keeps its own place and animation (no poses, plays or removes from the others) and takes no kill or hurt. It catches up when the event ends. Not a block. The flag is read each frame, and no reading counts as no event, so it cannot stick on by itself.

Not covered here: the dungeon flags held while in a dungeon (dungeon-live). That is the same idea for flags, applied by the flag store, not by actor.

## Moving platforms (branch `platforms-b`, issues 196 and 194)

A tip from a developer working on the same game (Hot Onion, shared by Alex, 2026-11-12): the game moves its platforms by delta time, like everything else, so syncing one variable puts a platform at the same place for every player; add a clock so packet latency is taken into account. `lichii/hook/coop_platforms.{h,cpp}` does that, with `PLATFORM_CLOCK` (type 400, [protocol.md](protocol.md), "Moving platforms").

**What drives them** (read from the game's data, `Pack/Actor/*.pack.zs` and `Banc/*_Static.bcett.byml.zs`, 2026-11-12; checked unless marked):

| Platform | Actor | Driver and its parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Deku Tree basement floor (#196) | `Obj_Deku_MoveFloor_A_01` (`_A_02` has no group and never moves) | `GroundControlActor_WaveLoop` in the same placement group (blackboard `WaveCycle_Linear`, `WaveCycle_Angular`, `WaveRadius_Linear`, `WaveRadius_Angular`: a wave of a fixed period). The two other controllers in that room's log move other things (fable-g3-objects, read from the Banc's `Phive.Placement.GroupGUID`): `GroundControlActor_ResidentMoveTo` (no blackboard; logic node `ActorLogicGroundControlResidentMoveTo`) moves the dynamic water plane when its group's flag changes ([dungeon.md](dungeon.md), "Movers with a group flag"), `GroundControlActor_Rotate` (`RotateSpeedX`, `RotateAccelX`) turns the thorn stick |
| Deku Tree compass room lifts (#176, #194) | `Obj_InsideTheDekuTree_ElevatorLift_A_01` to `_A_03` (and `ElevatorLiftLim_*`) | On/off animation (`ASCommand_OnOff`) and the blackboard's `StopTime`, `SlowDownTime` ([dungeon.md](dungeon.md)); the AS slot 0 frame is read as a candidate |
| Dodongo's Cavern room 1 up/down lifts | `Obj_DodongoCave_MoveFloor_A_01` (three: 10.5 96.5 -23.0, -10.5 96.5 -23.0, 0.0 96.5 -12.6), `_A_02` | Their own AI (`UpDownLiftMove` module): a physics slider with a motor (`MaxMoveSpeed`, `MinMoveSpeed`, `MoveForce`, `DecelDistance`) and a wait counter (`WaitFrame`, `SwitchWaitFrame`); one `GroundControlActor_SimpleMoveTo` (`LinearVelX` 1.66, `TargetPosX` 12.25) beside them |
| Others the same code covers (not looked at one by one) | `Obj_InsideJabu_Jabu_ElevatorLift_*`, `Obj_InsideJabu_Jabu_MoveFloor_A_01`, `Obj_ForestTemple_Elevator_A_01`, `Obj_FireTemple_MoveFloor_*`, `Obj_ShadowTemple_MoveFloor_*`, `*_RotateFloor_*`, `*_RotateBlade_*`, `Obj_MoveSpike_*`, `Obj_InsideGanonCastle_ExtendingPlatform_A_01`, `Land_GerudoValley_lift_A_01`, `Land_SpiritTemple_ElevatorMirror_A_01` / `ElevatorPit_A_01`, `GrouondControlActor_DiffentSpeedMoveTo` (the game's spelling) | as above: a `GroundControlActor_*` driver or the platform's own AI |

**Which word is the clock** is not in the data (it is in the logic nodes' native code), so the hook finds it in the running game. Four times a second it copies each driver within 40 m of our Link (the 8 nearest; the actor +0..+0x600, the first 0x200 bytes of each component, and the AS slot 0 frame), as floats. A float that steps at the same rate in three looks in a row (within 8 %, between 0.05 and 2,000 a second, heap pointers and the actor's own place left out) is a candidate; after three such checks the one whose rate reads as seconds or frames (1, 30 or 60 a second) is taken, else the steadiest. When it goes back past where it was after stepping at its rate, the jump is its cycle (a loop), and it is kept. A clock that stands still (a pause, a hitch) is held but not sent, and let go after 10 s; a stall never counts as a loop. The owner sends that word's key, value, rate and cycle; the others write the word at that key in their own copy of the same driver (same placement hash). A word the game puts back after each write (four writes in a row that did not hold) is no longer followed in that visit.

**Owner**: the game that has sent the clock longest without a 3 s break (a game that comes later follows; two at once: the lower player number). So a platform keeps its place for the players already riding it.

**Switches**: `LICHII_COOP_PLATFORMS=0` turns it off, `LICHII_COOP_PLATFORMS=probe` finds and sends but never writes (logs only). On by default. Logs: `coop: platforms: <hash> <name>: clock <where>, <rate> a second, cycle <c>: ours to send`, `coop: platforms: <hash> <name>: following player N's clock <where> (ours .., theirs .., .. s off)`, each write `coop: platforms: <hash> <where> <ours> -> <theirs>` (200 lines a session), and `... our writes of <where> do not hold (4 in a row): stopped following`.

**While our Link rides it** (issue 194): a follower does not write a platform's clock while our Link stands on it, nor does an owner put its own clock back after a pause then: a write moved the platform, and Link with it, in one frame (the rubber-band on the Deku Tree's timed platforms). Riding is the game's own word, the platform AI's bool plug `RideOnPlayer` (`CoopLogicProbe::Read`, as `coop_lift_ride.h`), read with who else is on it: the plug is also 1 while another player's puppet rides our copy, so a plug of 1 is ours unless another player stands on the platform actor's footprint (1.8 m round, 0.5 m under to 2.5 m over) and our Link does not (on a platform wider than the footprint, our Link on its far edge is still riding). With no plug the footprint alone. The plug is 0 while Link is in the air, so riding lasts 1 s after the last look that said so (`kRideGraceUs`): a jump on the platform keeps the hold; a `GroundControlActor_` controller is not where its floor is, so only its plug. Once he is off, the next write puts it in step (a held write is not counted as one the game took back). Logs: `<hash> <name>: our Link rides it (RideOnPlayer|footprint): its clock is left <s> s off until he is off` and `<hash> <name>: our Link is off it: its clock may be put in step again`.

**The water room's floor is a wave, and its phase is the clock** (deku-switches-b, the 0.10.1 Longshot playtest: "the moving platform in the water room is not synced"). The Deku Tree room G floor `Obj_Deku_MoveFloor_A_01` shares a Banc group (8932) with `GroundControlActor_WaveLoop` (`WaveCycle_Linear` 18 s, `WaveRadius_Linear` 4 m on x). Read from main: the WaveLoop controller's setup (main+0x2fff730) reads the four wave keys from the blackboard (Murmur3 hashes `0x0a6d2c57` cycle linear, `0x97c33c58` radius linear) into a wave block at controller + 192 (main+0x11d07b0: cycles at +8 and +32, radii at +20 and +44, three floats each) and puts the phase angles after them (+56 linear x, y, z, then angular): each from the place the floor starts at, wrapped into [0, 2 pi). It then goes on by 2 pi / cycle a second. Both games start it from the placed position, so the phase differs only by when each game loaded the room: that phase is the one value to share. The finder takes a word that steps at 2 pi / the float 48 bytes before it (`WavePhaseCycle`) before any seconds or frames word, with its loop 2 pi known at once (no wrap needs to be seen first), so the follower's write is that phase, as the owner's plus the server-time gap. Not known until a run: whether the block is inside the 0x200 bytes of a component the probe reads (+0xf8 from the controller if the controller is the component; a controller held by pointer is not read, and the probe lines then show no `GroundControlActor_WaveLoop ... clock` line).

**The phase is in the controller's node, not in a component** (#196, branch `platform-phase`, read from the recompiled main of build e0f3ccfaf50065ae). The runs answered the question above: no word of the WaveLoop actor or its components is the phase, and the floor's words (`c+0x60+0x1d8` at -26.4 and the like) are worked out again from it every frame, so no write of them held. The controller is an object of its own, the GroundControl node: `[node]` = main+0x4b6ad78 (its vtable), `[node + 8]` = the actor (its per-frame step main+0x2fff930 reads `[[node + 8] + 0x270] + 0x118`, the actor's logic), the wave block at node + 0xc0 (its destructor main+0x3000e10 puts the block's own vtable there). main+0x2fff930 calls the block's step main+0x386c160 every frame, which adds frame time x 2 pi / cycle to each phase and wraps it: linear x, y, z at node + 0xf8, 0xfc, 0x100 (cycle 48 bytes before), angular at node + 0x104 .. 0x10c (cycle 36 bytes before); the floor is then put at its start + radius x sin(phase) (main+0x3c42460). `GroundControlActor_Rotate`'s node is the same kind (vtable main+0x4b6acd8; setup main+0x2ffea40 -> main+0x11d0d80, step main+0x2ffed30 -> main+0x386bb00): its angle at node + 0xd4 goes on by its speed (node + 0xd0) and wraps at 2 pi, either way round. No hook names these nodes (patch 0029 hooks only the push node's start), so `GroundNode::Find` looks for the node from the actor: the pointer words of the actor, its table, its components and what they point to, breadth first, 4 objects deep, at most 4096 objects, once per controller (again every 5 s, 6 times, when not found). The node's words 0xc0 .. 0x13c go to the finder under key regions 0xfffe (wave) and 0xfffd (rotate), where the layout alone says which word is a phase (`NodePhaseCycle`); the [0, 2 pi) guard stays. A follower writes the owner's phase (plus the server-time gap) into its own node, the word the game itself steps, so it holds and the floor follows. The log says where the node was found (`its wave node at ... (actor+0x270 > ...)`) or that it was not. Every WaveLoop and Rotate controller shares this one path (coop_room_objects.cpp lists them: the WaveLoop and the Rotate of the Deku Tree water room, the Fire Temple's three Rotates, Inside Ganon's Castle's Rotate; the Gerudo Training Ground's Rotate has a wired input, RotateAxisX, so its own logic drives it and it is left alone). NOT covered yet: `GroundControlActor_ResidentMoveTo` (Fire Temple, Forest Temple, Jabu-Jabu, Deku Tree, Kakariko) and the SimpleMoveTo / DiffentSpeedMoveTo ones: their node is another class (one node class with vtable main+0x4b6ae68 and setup main+0x30006e0 has no wave or angle block), still to be read.

**Which words are never followed** (#196 guard, review-platform-phase-r1). The finder drops a wave word for good when it stands outside [0, 2 pi) or steps back other than by looping (from past the middle of its loop to before it) or by a reset to its start (under 0.1 rad: a switch or reload). A following game gives up on a driver only when its own finder dropped that word, or the word is a wave phase by the game's layout (a cycle word 48 bytes before it, or a node phase) and its own copy is outside [0, 2 pi). Other looping clocks (AS frames of lifts) are never judged by the owner's loop: that loop is an estimate from the one jump the owner saw.

### Moving platforms: own logic first, no guessed words (branch `room-objects`, issues 196, 249, 194)

**The rule:** in a game that follows another, a room object moves only through its own logic. That means the input its own switch, sensor or rider would set. CoopEyeSwitch sets the plate's plug, which drives the Deku Tree compass-room lifts' `IsOn`. CoopLiftRide sets `RideOnPlayer` (Jabu-Jabu's lifts and moving floor, the Forest Temple's elevators). The push block's own node starts again ([world.md](world.md), issue 147). Nothing is written into such an object from outside.

**Why:** the clock-word path above wrote what its finder guessed into the same objects, and the runs show what it guessed:
- On the Deku lifts it took a frame counter at 58 to 65 a second (`c+0x88+0x140`), and the game put each write back (`do not hold`, hs-deku-platforms-probe 2026-11-14).
- On the water room's WaveLoop it took `actor+0x388` at -0.432 a second. That is the controller's place (4 m, 18 s), not its phase (2 pi / 18 = 0.349 a second).
- On Jabu-Jabu's moving floor it took a word at -0.066 a second (hs-logic-probe 2026-11-15).

Two drivers then fought over one object, the follower's own logic and our write: the snaps of issue 194 and the out-of-step Jabu-Jabu platforms of issue 249.

**What is built:**
- `ClockFinder::Trusted`: only a word known to be a clock may be chosen, that is a wave mover's phase (`WavePhaseCycle`) or the AS slot 0 frame. Other stepping floats are still watched, but never sent or written.
- `PlatformSync::OwnLogic`: a driver with a live `RideOnPlayer` plug (a rider lift) is skipped, and so is a driver whose placement the romfs marks as driven (`coop_room_objects.{h,cpp}`). Neither is sent nor written. The log says it once a scene: `coop: platforms: <hash> <name>: driven by its own logic (<why>): its clock is not shared`.
- The table comes from `tools/puppet_pack/room_objects.py --romfs <dump>`. It lists all 132 placements of the platform names (`IsDriver`), 24 of them driven. Each line gives why it is driven: the room graph's wired inputs (switch, flag, sensor or event), `RideOnPlayer` (its logic type has the plug), or `with <name>` (a part with no logic, placed on a driven one: the Deku `ElevatorLiftLim_A_0n`). Inputs that only reset or warp a mover (`ForceWarp`, `IisForceWarp`, `Logic_*`, `RespawnTrigger`) do not drive it. A live actor is the placement of its name nearest to it in its scene.
- `CoopLiftRide::IsRideLift` also takes every name whose logic has `RideOnPlayer` (`CoopRoomObjects::RiderName`). That adds `Obj_ForestTemple_Elevator_A_01`, which runs the base lift's logic.

**Still on the clock path:** free-running movers only, those with no wired input and no rider plug. Examples are the Deku Tree's water-room wave (`GroundControlActor_WaveLoop`, whose phase is the only difference) and animation-looped platforms (AS frame). Looping pause counters stay on their own path below.

- The others hold the same plug on their own switch with the game's set-bool every frame while it is on. Their own timer (`HoldGeneralTimerSwitch`), delays and gates then run, and their movers move.
- The 12 plugs (`CoopRoomObjects::SwitchPlugs`, the generator's third table) are every switch output a room object reads that no CoopEyeSwitch line covers:
  - Dodongo: the moving ground and shortcut plate, two fire-wall eyes, the room 12 shutter plate.
  - Forest: the lamp, air wall and light plate, and the torch plate.
  - GTG: the eye-switch shutter.
  - Water: the two eye-switch shutters, the hit-switch shutter, and the fountain's `OnState` and `OffState`.

Plugs that also raise an event asker (g3-objects; owner 07:40 UTC 2026-11-17: a gimmick event gives the result only, no replay) carry guard flags: held only while they are all on in our game, so our asker can no longer fire. A guard the asker reads only when its room loads (a gate with `UpdateOnce` 1; the generator writes it `load:<flag>`) counts only if it was on at that load: the Sharer keeps its value from the switch's first sight after a room load (a new switch actor, or the first sight after a walk without it; not by time: a pause menu stops the walks mid-visit). The one such guard is the Deku Tree compass room's `RoomD_StepSwitch_On_01` (its asker is deleted by it only at the load, `Room_D_Prefab_b6c3`): landing live from the other player mid-visit it arms nothing (holding the plate would make our asker ask the event again); after a reload with it on, the plate is held and the lifts rise. Mid-visit the lifts' result hangs on our asker's `EventBool` alone, and that is NOT proven to work: the asker's own AI (`AI/EventRequestActor.root.ainb`) writes `EventBool`, `EventBoolEX1` and `EventFinish` 0 every tick until its own trigger rises. In big-run-0115 (deku-platforms-b21) the follower's game played the event's camera through its asker, its lifts joined the event but never went on, and its asker was not deleted. Row 98(2) (g1-events) sets that `EventBool` with the game's set-bool instead of the replay; whether the room graph sees it is what the `events-next-a-9-hs-event-result` probe checks, with a screenshot of both games. Every `UpdateOnce` gate in the romfs was checked (15 in 14 room graphs): no other event asker reads a flag that way.

Logs:
- `coop: switch share: <switch> <plug> at x y z: our player's press: sent`
- `...: room loaded with its load guard on: our asker is gone` / `...off: our asker stays, not held this visit`

### Moving platforms: the pause counters (branch `platforms-b2`)

Thanks to **Hot Onion** (same game build, shared by Alex, 2026-11-12), who found and measured this: the looping platforms (Dodongo's Cavern's up/down lifts and pillars) are not moved by a clock word but by their AI graph, a move, a pause, a move, a pause. Each pause is an `ExecuteGenericCounter` node, and the moves take the same time in every game, so shifting one pause by the gap puts the platform in phase for good (Hot Onion measured a height gap of 7.53 m going to 0.16 m, and staying). `lichii/hook/coop_counters.{h,cpp}`, `coop_counter.s`, `PLATFORM_COUNTER` (type 401, [protocol.md](protocol.md), "Moving platforms").

| What | Where (build e0f3ccfaf50065ae) | Checked |
|---|---|---|
| Counter start, called each time the graph enters the node (x0 = node) | main+0x5e6760; it begins `pacibsp`, `str d8, [sp, #-0x30]!`, ..., `ldr x8, [x8, #0x140]; blr x8` (the base length getter) | Hot Onion; the words checked in our dump |
| Node vtable | main+0x499f200 (+0x140 base length getter main+0x260c0e0, +0x148 random part) | Hot Onion; the slots read in our dump |
| Frames left, length | node +0x20, +0x2c (f32, 60 a second) | Hot Onion |
| The actor | [[[node + 8] + 8] + 0x1f0] | Hot Onion |

- **Code mods (issue 136)**: the hooked word main+0x5e6760 and the bytes of the cave and the ring's address are in `CoopSites::ChangedSite`, so a game mod that changes any of them turns co-op off at load, as for the other caves (the loader log: Skipping "coop puppet": main+... is not the game's own).
- **The emulator**: the loader puts `b 0x3c46700` over the `pacibsp` at main+0x5e6760 (`coop counters` in the loader log; not with `LICHII_COOP_PLATFORMS=0`). The cave (`coop_counter.s`, 40 bytes at main+0x3c46700, clear of the AI hold's table and of cave-move's and Fritchii's Ctrl+U spots) writes the node into a ring of 256 in the game's heap (every AI counter of the scene passes through it; 64 could be overrun in a busy room), does the `pacibsp` and goes back. Code pages cannot be written by the game, so the ring is made with the game's malloc on the calc thread (a caller at the svc cave, order 63) and its address put at main+0x3c46780 (0 until then: the cave only does the `pacibsp`). Needs the svc cave (puppets, spawn or live events on, as in every kit).
- **Read** (present thread, with the objects walk): each node the ring named whose vtable is the counter's and whose actor is in the walk and named `Obj_*` (not enemies, NPCs, items, Link). Its pause began (length - frames left) / 60 s ago. Two periods in a row between starts within 8 %: it loops. One node may time both waits of a lift whose way up and way down take different times: then the periods go A, B, A, B, the loop is A + B, and every game compares the start that ends the longer period (else one game's top wait could be lined up with the other's bottom wait). Once looping it stays so until three starts in a row fit neither. A platform moved once by a switch never loops and is left to the switch's flag.
- **Key, the same in every game**: the actor's placement hash, the pause's length x10 and the node's place in its graph (node - [node + 8]), mixed. Two of our nodes with one key are neither sent nor followed. Hot Onion's key used the base and random lengths from the getters; ours reads the length the node holds (needs no game call), so a random-length pause gets a new key each time and is never followed (untested either way).
- **Sync**: every 3 s each game sends its looping nodes (key, period, time since the last start, starts seen) with the server time. A game whose pause began more than 0.3 s from another game's start of the same key (the short way round the period) and that has seen fewer starts (a tie: the higher player number) changes its frames left once in that pause: shorter by the gap when it has that long left, else longer by the rest of the period (or by the gap when it began earlier). In phase, nothing is written; a pause menu or a hitch is put right at the next pause. Not with `LICHII_COOP_PLATFORMS=probe` (logged only).
- **The clock-word path above leaves an actor alone as soon as one of its counters is seen** (looping yet or not; for the rest of the scene), so the two never fight over one platform. In the big run of 2026-11-12 (integrate-p12 f4328a3) the Dodongo lifts' counters never counted as looping (no `loops every` line), the clock-word path took a physics place of the lifts (`c+0x60+0x104`, 0.67 a second) and the game put B's writes back (`do not hold`). The `GroundControlActor_*` drivers (WaveLoop, Rotate, ResidentMoveTo, SimpleMoveTo) have no counter and stay on the clock-word path.
- **What the ring caught**: every 10 s while a platform counter is near (60 lines a session), `coop: platforms: pause counters: <n> starts in 10 s (<m> looks overran the ring), <k> new nodes now, <p> platform nodes, <o> other actors' nodes, <l> looping: <name> <left>/<length> ...`. No such line at all: the ring is empty (the cave or the caller). Platform nodes 0: no counter of an `Obj_` actor started near Link.
- **Hookshot**: patch `hookshot/patches/0003-coop-platforms.patch` (after 0001 and 0002): `nx2/coop_platform_lift.py` puts C after `mov x19, x0` at main+0x5e6778 (words and build id checked; called from `nx2/coop_hooks.py` install()) and `host/coop_platform_ring.c` writes x0 into the same ring at the address main+0x3c46780 holds. The module makes the ring the same way (the game's malloc through the frame hook's calls). Hookshot's build log says `co-op platforms: pause counter hook at main+0x5e6778`.
- Logs: `coop: platforms: pause counter ring at <address>` (or `no counter cave ...: pause counters off`), `<hash> <name>: pause counter <key> loops every <s> s`, `<hash> <name>: pause counter <key> <gap> s off player N's: frames left <before> -> <after>`.
- **Lifts whose moves take different times** (report 138, Dodongo's Cavern with 4 players): the lifts are physics sliders, so a rider or a slower frame makes a move longer, and the three games' periods for one lift came out 21, 26, 29, 33 and 54 s. No two agreed within 8 %, so no game followed another (the 0.11.4 logs have `loops every` lines and no `off player` line on the host), and the lifts drifted. Now (review of #279):
  - **One leader, the same in every game:** for the pause-by-pause rule below, the lowest player number of the games that send the counter's waits. Counts compared at different times could leave two games each thinking it leads (review of #279), so the number decides.
  - **Each wait on its own key.** A pause starts at the lift's home wait (within `kHomeM`, 0.5 m, of its placement in the room table, the same in every game) or at its far one, and every counter seen starting `kPauseStarts` (3) times with its wait known is sent per wait (`WaitKey`: the time between two starts of that wait, and its last start), looping or not. A switch's one move never reaches 3 starts. The placement is found once per counter node for the scene, by its horizontal place (a lift moves up and down; Dodongo's three are 15 m apart), so it never flips to another placement of the same name. The distance from it is logged at a node's first two pauses (`<hash> <name>: a pause <m> m from its placement (home|far wait)`), to check the 0.5 m in players' logs.
  - **Pause by pause (`AlignPause`):** when our wait is known and the leader sends its waits, our pause of a wait ends when the leader's pause of that wait does, from the leader's actual start of it (no older than one pause before ours), every pause. A start guessed from the leader's last period was off by seconds on a physics lift (review of #279), so when the leader's start has not come yet (we began first, or it is more than a pause ahead: its next start of that wait is most of a period away), our pause is held from running out (kept above `kKeepFrames`) until it comes, at most `kMaxHoldUs` (60 s), then ends with the leader's. Logs: `<hash> <name>: pause counter <key> held for player N's start (home|far wait)`, `... <gap> s off player N's (this pause, home wait|far wait): frames left <before> -> <after>`.
  - **The period rule above** stays for a counter with no placement known (its two waits can't be told apart) and for a leader that sends no waits (an older module).
  - **Movers their own logic drives** (the room table's `why`: a switch, a sensor, a rider) are never sent or written: a lift ridden many times is no loop to follow.
  - **Send rate:** a start that goes into the message is sent at once, at most one message every 250 ms (`kMinSendGapUs`); the rest every 3 s as before. More entries than one message holds (32) go in more messages, so no counter always misses out.
  - **A pause we made longer** reads above its length until it runs down: that is no misread any more (it dropped the counter, which started over at zero starts and was never lined up again; the period rule's longer pauses hit it too).
  - Tests (`counters_test.cpp`): moves of 6.0 to 10.1 s in one game; a leader whose moves vary from 6.1 to 11.3 s; offsets of 5 to 16 s in a 21 s cycle; equal counts with the higher number ahead (no deadlock); three games where two agree on a period and the leader's differs; half a cycle apart (never top on bottom); a driven mover; a counter restarting every 0.15 s; no wait keys without a wait; twelve lifts in more than one message.
- Not covered: rail movers (`ExecuteRailMove`, the spikes), which are continuous; the clock-word path may still find their word.
- **Pause menus, late arrivals and the shortcut lift** (tracker row 164, the 0.11.6 Dodongo playtest: the lifts stayed down for a long time at random, a pause or quick-switch menu put them out of step, and leaving and coming back did not help). The logs showed three causes:
  - **The shortcut lift was never lined up.** Of the three room 1 lifts, the one at 10.5 96.5 -23.0 has the wired input `IsShortCutMove` (the step switch's shortcut), so the room table marked it driven and its counters were never sent or written (`driven by its own logic (IsShortCutMove)` on every visit in the host's log). That input comes from a switch every game shares (`SWITCH_STATE`), so the lift runs the same logic everywhere and loops like its two neighbours. Now only a player's own input keeps a mover out (`CoopRoomObjects::PlayerDriven`: `RideOnPlayer`, `RideOnCharacter`, `IsInPlayer`, `IsTargetFloor`: the rider lifts of Jabu-Jabu and the Forest Temple, player sensors). A mover with any other wired input (`Seen::switched`: `IsShortCutMove`, `IsOn`, `MoveToPosX`, ...) is sent and lined up once it loops on its own (two periods alike), so a switch's moves never are.
  - **A game standing still led the others.** The leader was the lowest player number (the host), also while its game stood still in a pause menu, a load or a cutscene. Each follower then held its lift at a wait for the host's next start, up to more than a period (the guest's log: `held for player 1's start` 137 times, pauses ended 11 to 27 s late). Now the players' `PLAYER_STATUS` (`paused`, `in_menu`, `loading`, `in_cutscene`) goes into the counter sync: an away game leads nobody (neither rule), and our own game writes nothing while it is away (its frames stand still while the server clock runs).
  - **A game that came back pulled the others, and followed late.** A game that returns or comes into the room leads only once it has sent its counters for `kSettleUs` (45 s) without being away (the times are the server times of its own messages, the same in every game). Two games agree on who is settled only once both have been in the scene that long: in the first 45 s of a game in the scene, its view of the others' times may differ (it saw none of their messages yet), so the leader can change once then. Until then it follows, and it follows from its first pause there (before: its third, about a minute), as the leader shares only a counter that repeats in its game. A pause is left alone when it ends within `kAlignOffS` (0.15 s, was 0.3 s) of the leader's.
  - Tests (`counters_test.cpp`, `room_objects_test.cpp`): the shortcut lift lined up; a switched mover pressed at no steady rate never written; the leader in a pause menu for 25 s holds nobody and, back, follows instead of pulling; our own game away writes nothing; a lower number coming into the room follows at once and never pulls the settled game.
  - The same code covers every looping counter lift (Fire, Shadow, Spirit movers with a placement). The rider lifts (Jabu-Jabu, the Forest Temple elevators) stay each player's own (`CoopLiftRide`), as before. Not covered: a leader whose game runs slower than the others (its lifts fall behind each cycle); the followers then still hold at a wait for its start.

**Not known until a run**: whether the word found is the one the platform is drawn from (a WaveLoop's phase should be; a ResidentMoveTo that steps its place toward a target may have no clock, only a place, which `WORLD_MOVE` already shares, [world.md](world.md)), and whether the Dodongo lifts' physics slider takes a written position or the AI moves it back (then the "do not hold" line comes and nothing is lost). Triggered platforms (a switch moves them once) are followed only while their clock steps in the owner's game; one that has come to rest is left to the switch's flag. Shared by both runtimes (the hook code is the same in Hookshot; nothing in `hookshot/` needed).
